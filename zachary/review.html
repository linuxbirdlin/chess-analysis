Pattern Analysis

The Sicilian Weakness — Confirmed Across 3 Events

Zachary's accuracy drops dramatically in sharp Sicilian middlegames — both as White and as Black:

  • CC G266854 (B46 Taimanov as Black, vs Tseng 1907): 79.4% accuracy, 4 blunders — loss
  • PAM R4 (B75 Dragon Yugoslav as White, vs Piza 1766): 83.4% accuracy, 4 blunders — unknown result but nearly certainly losing

  • BC Cup G262643 (A06 Reti, vs Mu 2038): 89.0% accuracy, 2 blunders — still won, but significantly below par
In non-Sicilian positions: Zachary's average accuracy is ~95%. In sharp Sicilian pawn-storm positions: average drops to ~84%. This is a 10+ percentage point degradation that represents a systematic vulnerability, not random variation.

Never Lost as White — A Pattern to Break

Zachary has never lost as White in this dataset (13W–0L–8D). His White record is near-perfect. Austin, playing against Zachary's White, must play to create maximum imbalance as Black. Playing solidly into a Reti/English endgame against Zachary as White will almost certainly end in a draw at best, loss at worst.

Draw-Seeking with Lower-Rated Opponents

Zachary drew PAM R1 vs a 1645-rated player and PAM R3 vs a 1833-rated player. Both times, he had slight advantages and chose simplification. This pattern suggests he defaults to drawing mode when he perceives the position as "safe enough." Austin's opening prep as Black should specifically avoid giving Zachary this "safe" feeling.

Elite Endgame Technique

The 97.4% accuracy / 104-move draw against Keleberda (2512) is a benchmark. Zachary's endgame defensive technique is at a very high level. Austin must avoid technical endgames — any simplification that reaches a rook-pawn endgame strongly favors Zachary regardless of material balance.
