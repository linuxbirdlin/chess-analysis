Part 1 — Austin as White

What Justin will play

Justin plays the French Defense in every single game against 1.e4. Across 7 documented 1.e4 games, he responded with 1...e6 100% of the time. Austin will face the French.

Justin's French knowledge is deep — he has played it versus the Exchange, Classical, Advanced, and Burn variations, scoring wins in the Advanced and Burn lines, and holding firm draws in the Exchange.

What Austin tried before — and why it failed

In their NAYCC encounter, Austin played the French Advanced (C02): 1.e4 e6 2.d4 d5 3.e5 c5 4.c3 Nc6 5.Nf3 Nge7 6.dxc5...

Critical error sequence:

  • Move 14: Austin played 14.b4 with queenside expansion — premature before the center was stabilized.
  • Move 15: Austin played 15.f5 — aggressive but it gave Justin the e5 square.
  • Move 19: After 19.Bxc5 Ne4 — Austin let Justin post a dominant knight on e4 and couldn't dislodge it.
  • Moves 43–50: Justin's connected d-pawn and e-pawn rolled: ...d3, ...e4, ...e3, ...Qd2, ...Qf2# — a passed pawn avalanche Austin didn't stop.
Root cause: Austin pushed b4 and f5 without a clear forcing plan, giving Justin tempos to centralize. Do NOT repeat this.

Option A — French Tarrasch (3.Nd2) Primary recommendation

Justin has never faced the Tarrasch in any reviewed game. It avoids sharp theory and gives Austin a flexible structure without committing to the e5 advance early.

1.e4 e6 2.d4 d5 3.Nd2 Nf6 4.e5 Nfd7 5.Bd3 c5 6.c3 Nc6
7.Ne2 cxd4 8.cxd4 f6 9.exf6 Nxf6 → Nf4 targeting d5

Key ideas:

  • After ...f6 exf6, play Nf4 targeting d5, then O-O, Qe2, Rd1 — slow pressure
  • Justin's knights (Nc6, Nf6) will want e4 or d5. Prevent this with Ne5 or Nf4
Avoid: b4 without securing the center · f5 without a forced sequence · allowing ...Ne4 as an outpost

Option B — King's Indian Attack (2.d3) Alternative

Completely sidesteps French theory. Justin has no recorded experience against the KIA.

1.e4 e6 2.d3 d5 3.Nd2 Nf6 4.Ngf3 c5 5.g3 Nc6 6.Bg2 Be7 7.O-O O-O

Key ideas:

  • Typical plan: Nh4-f5 or e4-e5 when Black releases tension with ...dxe4
  • If Justin plays ...d4, use Nf1-g3-f5 and kingside pressure
  • Watch for ...c4 — Justin's queenside space grab

What to exploit in Justin's French

  • Light squares: After ...Bxg3 or ...Nc6 structures, Justin's light-squared bishop is often passive. Target a passed pawn on d5 or open the e-file.
  • Kingside attacks: Justin lost to Velasquez (Canada Closed R8) via 1.e4 e6 2.d4 d5 3.Nc3 Nf6 4.e5 Nfd7 5.Nce2 c5 6.c3 Nc6 7.f4 — study this game.
  • Psychology: Justin's French draws against lower-rated players should not intimidate Austin. Preparation beats habit.

Part 2 — Austin as Black

What Justin will play

Justin plays 1.d4 exclusively as White, with QGD-based structures and d4-c4-Nf3-Nc3 setups. Occasionally the Benoni pawn structure when Black plays ...c5 early.

Recommended weapon: Nimzo-Indian (E32–E51)

1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 e6 3.Nc3 Bb4

This is the ideal choice because:

  • Austin has played it in 4+ games and is deeply familiar with the patterns
  • It forces Justin to commit 3.Nc3 early
  • The resulting IQP / ...Ne4 outpost / ...Ba6 positions are exactly where Austin is tactically dangerous

Specific variation: Bxc3+ with ...b6/...Ba6

1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 e6 3.Nc3 Bb4 4.e3 O-O 5.Bd3 d5
6.a3 Bxc3+ 7.bxc3 b6 8.Nf3 Ba6 9.cxd5 exd5
White gets isolated doubled pawns on c3/c4. Black gets the bishop pair. Austin's knight heads to ...Ne4 — his favorite square.

Key tactical ideas to have ready:

  • ...Nc5 hitting c4 and e4
  • ...Ne4 centralization followed by ...Qc8-g4 or ...Qd5 diagonal pressure
  • ...N4g3+ piece sacrifice when h2 is unguarded (Austin executed this in NAYCC R3)
  • If White plays a3 early, Austin has the ...c5 break to open the center

What to avoid

Do NOT play the Capablanca line (4.Qc2) without preparation — Justin's solid Bd3/Be2 + Nf3 setup can leave Austin passive if he trades on c3 too early.
Do NOT try a Benoni (1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 c5) — Justin plays 3.d5 and gains a space advantage. Stick to the Nimzo-Indian.
