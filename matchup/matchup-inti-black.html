The Single Most Important Rule

NEVER castle queenside (O-O-O) vs Inti's f4 system.
In Queretaro R6, De Alba played 11…O-O-O and gave Inti +3.0 advantage immediately. Inti specifically prepares for the queenside king — he'll play a4-a5 and rook lifts. Castle kingside in every variation.

Anti-f4-f5 Principles

When Inti pushes f4-f5

  • …e5 (close the kingside): If Austin has solid central control, close with …e5. This denies the f6 square to Inti's pieces and leaves him with no target.
  • …d5! (central counterattack): If Austin has better development and Nc3 is unstable, counterattack with …d5. This is most effective when …b4 has already displaced the Nc3.
In Queretaro R6, Inti's f4-e5 push only succeeded because Black (De Alba) made two consecutive blunders (11…O-O-O, 12…Nc5). Against accurate play, the f4-e5 push gives Black active counterplay.
