Part 1 — Austin as White vs Aarush

Austin's weapon: h4 English Attack vs Najdorf Aarush is 0W–3L against direct h4 kingside attacks. His three h4 losses: CC R1 (80.6%, 3 blunders), CC R2 (91.5%, h4 Najdorf Heye), Toronto R6 (84%, 4 blunders — including blundering from a won +7.31 position). The h4 system doesn't need to be objectively winning — it creates complexity that eats Aarush's clock and induces catastrophic single-move collapses even from winning positions.

Novelty finding — Aarush's Najdorf autopilot

After 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 d6 3.d4 cxd4, Aarush plays ...Nf6 in 100% of Lichess games in under 1 second — and after 4.Nxd4 Nf6 5.Nc3, he plays ...a6 100% of the time. He is on deep autopilot through the Najdorf move order. This makes 6.h4! even more effective: he expects 6.Bg5 or 6.Be2 (standard), not the immediate kingside pawn push. The clock data shows he slows down significantly once White deviates from his expected lines.
