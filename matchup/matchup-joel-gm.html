Part 2 — Austin as Black: Solid QGD Structure vs Nimzo-Larsen

The correct antidote: 1...d5 → solid QGD-type development → mandatory ...c5 break on move 6. Austin's Nimzo-Indian background (Bb4/...b6/...Ba6 structures, similar pawn formations) provides direct positional understanding for this exact setup. The ...c5 break is non-negotiable — without it, Austin drifts into passive defense.

Main line — QGD Solid vs Nimzo-Larsen

1.Nf3 d5 2.b3 Nf6 3.Bb2 e6 4.e3 Be7 5.d4 O-O 6.Bd3 c5! 7.O-O Nc6 8.Nbd2 b6
...c5 is the critical break — must be played on or before move 6. ...b6 prepares ...Ba6, fighting the long diagonal or expanding queenside.

Positional plan after 8...b6:

9.Ne5 Bb7 10.f4 cxd4 11.exd4 Rc8 12.Ndf3 Nd5

After ...cxd4 exd4, Black has the IQP position from the White side. ...Rc8 prepares ...Nc6/...Nd5 central activity. Austin's IQP experience from Nimzo-Indian structures applies directly — he knows how to handle this pawn structure. Black's Bb7 bishop becomes active after ...Nd5 creates space.

1.Nf3 d5 2.b3 Nf6 3.Bb2 e6 4.g3 c5! 5.Bg2 Nc6 6.O-O Be7 7.d4 cxd4
4...c5! immediately, before Joel builds the full fianchetto structure — Benoni-type counterplay.

If Joel plays 4.g3 (fianchetto variation), Austin plays 4...c5! immediately rather than 4...Be7. This Benoni-type response creates immediate tension before Joel's Bg2 is established. After ...cxd4, Black has active piece play and the center is already broken open.
