Critical Patterns — What Stockfish Revealed

G22 (White, Draw): mv35 Qh4 (+6.56→−6.69, cpl 1000) — a +6.56 position instantly turned into a loss; Bd3 was correct. These three decisive position flips are the most dangerous pattern. Marco can be clearly winning and lose the game on the very next move.
Pattern 2 — Endgame rook/king navigation blind spot (G7, G11, G20): In G7, Marco played Kd3 (instead of Rg1) three times in similar positions from +5.60 and +5.59, squandering winning rook endgames. In G11, he held a +24.87 advantage and played Re6 → Ra6 → Re2 when Re1 was correct all three times — drawing a completely won endgame through a single repeated error. In G20, he had +8.60 and played Kd4 (cpl 860) → 0.00, then immediately Kc5 (cpl 404) → −4.04 to lose a drawn endgame. This is a diagnosable, specific blind spot: Marco does not visualize rook placement behind passed pawns in K+R endgames.
Pattern 3 — Very high peak accuracy (97%+): Three games scored 97%+: G6 (White Win, 97.3%, 0 blunders), G9 (Black Win, 97.1%, 0 blunders), G10 (Black Win, 97.3%, 10 blunders but all in an already +70 position). When Marco is focused and positions are sharp, he plays at an IM-equivalent level. The 97.3% G6 win was a crisp Exchange Caro-Kann middlegame attack. Austin should not be overconfident — Marco is fully capable of outplaying him in a clean game.
Pattern 4 — Won-position draw failures (G11, G14, G19, G20): Beyond the decisive collapses, Marco drew from clearly winning positions in multiple additional games. G11: +24.87 → draw (rook blunders). G14: Missed forced mate at move 35 (Rd5 instead of Be6) → draw. G19: +5.46 → 0.03 (Qc7 instead of Bxc3) → draw. G20: +8.60 → draw → loss. This pattern is consistent: once Marco builds a large advantage, he tends to relax and make imprecise moves rather than finding the clinical finisher. Austin should keep all three results alive past move 25 and never resign in a losing position — Marco may well gift the draw or win.
Pattern 5 — Strongest as Black in complex tactical middlegames: Marco's best games (G9 97.1%, G10 97.3%, G8 94.3%) are all as Black in Sicilian-type positions with active counterplay. His 4W-3L-2D Black record (88.4% avg) is his most reliable color, particularly when he has dynamic piece play. Austin as White should avoid closed positional systems and steer toward open tactical play (Rossolimo exchange 4.Bxc6) where converting the initiative requires precise technique — Marco's conversion weakness applies equally to piece-play advantages.
