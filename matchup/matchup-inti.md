## Part 1 — Austin as White vs Inti's 1...e5

### Inti's Black Repertoire vs 1.e4

Inti plays **1...e5 exclusively**. His systems:

**vs Italian (3.Bc4):** C50 Giuoco Pianissimo and C54 Italian Classical — `3...Bc5 4.c3 Nf6 5.d3 d6`. He develops solidly with ...O-O, ...Re8, ...h6, and waits for White to overextend. In Queretaro R1, he found a tactical opportunity (...Nf4! exploiting an open kingside) after White erred.

**vs Ruy Lopez (3.Bb5):** C69 Exchange and C77/C78 Morphy — `3...a6 4.Bxc6` or `4.Ba4 Nf6 5.O-O b5 6.Bb3 Bc5`. He's more comfortable in Exchange Ruy (won Queretaro R3) than Morphy Defense (lost Agua R1 vs CM).

### Key Weakness to Target: Positional Squeeze

Inti's Black game breaks down when White establishes **long-term central pressure with a space advantage**. In Agua R1, the CM Lopez Calderon played d4 (at move 18), gained central space, and forced Inti into a passive defense — after which accuracy dropped sharply. Inti's Italian/Lopez as Black is tactical, not prophylactic. He seeks piece activity; if White contains his pieces, he becomes passive.

He does NOT collapse quickly in pure tactical complications (Queretaro R1 showed he finds tactics well). The weakness is strategic squeeze, not tactical defense.

### Recommended: Italian Game (Classical) with d4 central break

Austin should play the **Italian Game** — this is already part of Austin's repertoire and forces Inti into his most-studied territory, but where Austin can control the game tempo:

```
1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4 Bc5 4.c3 Nf6 5.d3 d6 6.O-O O-O 7.Re1
```

Then build with Nbd2, a4 (or h3 first to prevent ...Bg4), then d4 when ready.

**Why Italian over Lopez:**
1. Austin likely has Italian preparation already (it's the most common 1.e4 response to 1...e5)
2. Against Lopez, Inti knows both Exchange (C69) and Morphy (C77) — more prep coverage
3. Italian d4 break creates the exact space squeeze Inti struggles with (Agua R1 pattern)
4. Inti's Italian as Black (C54 Classical) showed he develops solidly but relies on White making errors — Austin should avoid giving him free pieces by playing principled d4 pressure

**Concrete plan after 7.Re1:**
```
7...a6 (or 7...a5) 8.a4 (preventing ...b5 queenside break)
8...Ba7 9.h3 (prevent ...Bg4 pin) 9...Re8 10.Nbd2 h6 11.Nf1-Ng3
```

The Ng3 maneuver pressures f5 and h5. If Black ever plays ...Nh7 (heading to f4), Austin plays g3 to deny the f4 square. The position builds slowly — exactly the type of patient squeeze Inti struggles with.

**When Inti plays ...d5 break:**
If Inti plays `7...d5!? 8.exd5 Nxd5 9.Nxe5 Nxe5 10.Rxe5 Nf6` — this is the Giuoco Piano central break. Austin should play 11.d4 Bb6 12.Bg5 — maintaining central tension. Inti handled this in Copa R5 but his opponent blundered; Austin should not.

### Alternative: Ruy Lopez with Re1-d4 plan

If Austin prefers the Lopez:
```
1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5 a6 4.Ba4 Nf6 5.O-O b5 6.Bb3 Bc5
```
This is Inti's Chigorin-type. Austin plays `7.c3 d6 8.d4 Bb6 9.Be3` — same d4 pressure idea. Inti's only Lopez loss was vs CM Lopez Calderon playing exactly this plan (d4 on move 18, building central space). Austin can try to reach this structure earlier.

## Part 2 — Austin as Black vs Inti's 1.e4

### Inti's White Repertoire vs Sicilian

Inti plays Open Sicilian (d4) in all known games against 1...c5:
- **B32/B35:** vs Dragon/Nc6 — `5.Nc3 g6 6.Be3 Bc4` or `5.Nb5 a6 6.Nd6+` (Löwenthal trick)
- **B43:** vs Kan/Taimanov `...a6 e6` — `5.Nc3 Qc7 6.Bd3 b5 7.Qe2 8.Bd2 9.f4` — f4-e5 push system

Austin plays Taimanov (1...c5, 2...e6, 3...Nc6, 5...a6). Against Taimanov specifically, Inti's data is thin — the only Kan-adjacent game (Queretaro R6, B43) featured `6.Bd3` not English Attack.

### What Inti Will Likely Play vs Taimanov

After `1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 e6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 Nc6 5.Nc3 a6`:

Based on his game vs Pablo (B43 Kan), Inti plays:
- `6.Bd3` — then `7.Qe2` or `f3` — building toward f4-e5

**OR** he may play `6.Be3` (English Attack — common at higher club level).

Inti's B43 game showed a specific weakness: after `...b4 Nb1 Nfxe4 Be3` he lost the positional thread and ran into time trouble. Austin should steer for lines where Black gets active piece play early.

### Recommended: Taimanov with ...b4 queenside pressure

Against `1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 e6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 Nc6 5.Nc3 a6 6.Bd3`:

```
6...Nf6 7.O-O Be7 8.Kh1 O-O 9.f4 d6 10.Nxc6 bxc6
```

This reaches a Scheveningen structure with ...cxd (open b-file), giving Austin queenside pressure. Black's ...c5 break later opens the game.

Against `6.Be3` (English Attack):
```
6...Qc7 7.Bd3 Nf6 8.O-O Be7 9.f4 d6 10.Kh1 O-O
```

Then `11...Nxd4 12.Bxd4 b5` — the ...b5 push is Austin's main weapon. In Queretaro R6, Inti's position fell apart after Black's ...b4 and ...bxc3 created connected queenside passers.

**Critical anti-pattern:** Do NOT play `...O-O-O` vs Inti's f4 system. In Queretaro R6, De Alba played 11...O-O-O?? and gave White a huge advantage. Inti specifically prepared for queenside castling — he has the a-file attack ready. Austin should castle kingside.

**Key anti-f4 principle:** When Inti pushes f4-f5, Austin answers ...e5 (closing the f-file) or prepares ...d5 counterplay. In Queretaro R6, Inti's f4-e5 push worked when Black gave him time — don't give him that time.

## Part 3 — Key Tactical Themes

### Themes Austin Must Be Ready For

**As White:**
- **Italian d4 central break:** Build Re1, Nbd2-Nf1-Ng3, then d4 when Black's pieces are passively placed. If Inti plays ...d5, meet with exd5 Nxd5 Nxe5 and recover piece activity.
- **h3 prophylaxis:** Always play h3 before Bg5 lines in Italian — Inti's ...Bg4 pinning the f3-knight (seen in Copa R1, Queretaro R1) is a recurring weapon.
- **...Nf4 tactical trigger:** In multiple games, Inti reached a knight-f4 fork motif. Austin should calculate if ...Nf4 is possible before releasing central tension.

**As Black:**
- **...b5-b4 queenside break:** Inti's key weakness in Sicilian positions is the queenside counterplay. Once Austin plays ...b5, push ...b4 to disrupt the Nc3 and open files.
- **Do NOT castle queenside:** Inti specifically looks for O-O-O from Black in f4 systems. Castle kingside.
- **...e5 as f-file closer:** If Inti pushes f4-f5, respond with ...e5 to close the kingside. Inti's Queretaro R6 game showed he doesn't know how to proceed when the kingside closes.

## Part 4 — Psychological and Practical Notes

**Inti is a rapid improver** — +256 FIDE in 13 months. This means he has almost certainly added new opening lines and corrected old weaknesses since the games we've analyzed. The Sep–Nov 2026 tournament data (during his biggest rating gain period) is missing. Treat the opening analysis as directional, not definitive.

**Inti plays in time trouble.** Copa blitz games showed consistent time pressure (0:00:06 remaining by move 24 in R4). Even in classical games, Queretaro R6 showed 3:47 remaining on move 12 vs opponent's 49:18. Inti calculates deeply but slowly — in G/90+30, he will often have less time than Austin in the critical phase.

**Inti fights when behind.** His only classical draw (Queretaro R5 vs Hernandez Gamboa, rated 1692) came from a +6.9 position that he should have won. He pressed too hard for the win in time trouble and let it slip. This suggests he doesn't play for draws — Austin should exploit over-pressing.

**Inti hasn't faced Austin's preparation.** Austin's Exchange Caro-Kann and Rossolimo are not part of Inti's game database. As White, Austin will be facing a 1...e5 player which is outside Austin's primary preparation zone — both sides will be in somewhat unfamiliar territory.

**Rating gap is small and irrelevant at this level.** 52 FIDE points is nothing. Austin has beaten players 100+ points higher. The decisive factor will be opening preparation and middlegame accuracy, not ratings.
