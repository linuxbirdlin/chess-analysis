Part 1 — Austin as White: Rossolimo + h4! Before the Fianchetto

Primary weapon: Rossolimo (3.Bb5). Tarun plays Sicilian 1...c5 → 2...Nc6 in every game vs 1.e4. After Rossolimo 3.Bb5, he responds 3...Nf6 4.Bxc6 dxc6 (accepting doubled c-pawns) and then fianchettoes with 5...g6 6...Bg7. The critical countermeasure: play 6.h4! immediately — before Black completes the fianchetto. Austin's h4 system is well-prepared from other matchups. This disrupts Tarun's entire plan.

Main line — Rossolimo Exchange + h4!

Austin: 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5! Nf6 4.Bxc6! dxc6 5.d3 g6 6.h4! — Tarun wants ...Bg7, ...b6, ...Ng4. h4! attacks the kingside immediately before the bishop lands on g7. Observed position: Skyline R4 vs Lin Benjamin 2099 went 5.d3 g6 6.c3 Bg7 — c3 allowed Black to fianchetto. Austin should avoid c3 and play h4 instead.
Austin: 5.d3 g6 6.h4! Bg7 7.h5! gxh5 8.Rxh5 O-O 9.Nc3! — h5 forces a concession. If 6...h6 instead (blocking), Austin plays 7.Nc3 Bg7 8.Be3 b6 9.Qd2 with long castling and f4 attack. If 6...h5 (supporting), Austin plays 7.g4! to break open the kingside anyway.
If 6...h6 (blocking h5): 7.Nc3! Bg7 8.Be3 b6 9.Qd2! O-O 10.O-O-O — castle queenside and unleash the f-pawn (f4-f5). Tarun's doubled c-pawns on c5/c6 are long-term endgame weakness. Austin targets c5 via d4-d5 pawn push.

After the h4 exchange, White gets open h-file and targets Black's compromised kingside structure. The doubled c-pawns (c5 backward, c6 isolated) become targets in the ending. Tarun's plan was built around fianchettoing smoothly — h4 denies him that.

Tarun has not been observed playing 3...e5. If he does: Austin plays 4.c3 → positional Rossolimo, keeping the bishop and maintaining the c3-d4 pawn break option.
Austin: 3.Bb5 e5 4.c3! a6 5.Ba4 b5 6.Bc2 Nf6 7.d4! — Lopez-type structure; d4 immediately creates central tension before Black's setup consolidates. The bishop retreated to c2 supports d4 push. Standard Rossolimo handling.
Austin: 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 e6 3.d4! cxd4 4.Nxd4 a6 5.Nc3 Qc7 6.Bd3! — Open Sicilian vs Kan/Taimanov. Tarun played 2...e6 once (Texas Grand Circuit R4 vs Pyrig 2395) responding to an unusual 3.b3. Against Austin's main 3.d4, Tarun will face an Open Sicilian rather than Rossolimo. Austin handles Taimanov structures from other prep (vs Yaen, vs Daniel Lee).

Part 2 — Austin as Black: Navigating the English + Mikenas Danger

Core fact: Tarun plays 1.c4 English exclusively as White. Austin does not normally play vs 1.c4 — this requires specific preparation. The Mikenas Attack (2.Nc3 Nf6 3.e4) is Tarun's sharpest weapon, reserved for strong opponents. Austin must know the Mikenas response cold. The Catalan-English (2.g3) is manageable — it resembles 1.d4 Catalan structures Austin knows from Nimzo prep.

Austin's recommended Black move order

Tarun: 1.c4 Nf6 2.Nc3 e6 — This move order lets Tarun choose: Mikenas (3.e4) or Catalan-English (3.g3) or other. Austin's response is prepared for all three. Do NOT play 1...e5 early — that plays into Tarun's English structure as reversed Sicilian-type which he knows well.
Mikenas is Tarun's most dangerous line. He used it against IM Fus (2419) at Texas Grand Circuit — his most ambitious White weapon. Austin must know the Black response cold or face an unfamiliar pawn structure with Tarun fully prepared.
Tarun: 1.c4 Nf6 2.Nc3 e6 3.e4 c5! 4.e5 Ng8! 5.Nf3 Nc6 6...d6! — Classic Mikenas Black response: 3...c5! challenges the center immediately; 4.e5 Ng8 retreats the knight but threatens ...d6 to undermine the e5 wedge. 6...d6! is the principled break: challenge e5 at once before White consolidates with d4.
After 6...d6! 7.d4 cxd4: 8.Nxd4 Nxe5! 9.Nxe5 dxe5 10.Ndb5! — Black wins the e5 pawn back after 7.d4 cxd4 8.Nxd4 Nxe5 — positional compensation for the knight retreat. After 10.Ndb5 White targets d6; Austin plays 10...Bb4! pinning the knight. Complex position with mutual counterplay — exactly what Austin needs vs Tarun.

Alternative response: 3...d5!? (central challenge) — 3.e4 d5!? 4.cxd5 exd5 5.exd5 Nxd5 6.Nxd5 Qxd5 — simplifies immediately but leads to equal endgame positions Tarun can handle. Prefer 3...c5! for maximum complexity.

Tarun: 1.c4 Nf6 2.g3 e6 3.Bg2 d5! 4.Nf3 Be7 5.O-O O-O 6...Ne4! — Queen's Indian themes translate here: ...d5 occupies the center, ...Ne4 centralizes powerfully and threatens ...Nxc3. After 7.d3 Nxg3 8.hxg3 dxc4 9.dxc4 Austin has traded off the knight pair and opened the position. Or 7.d4 — Austin plays standard Nimzo-Indian ...Bd7/...Ba4 type plans.
Alternative (Bogo-Indian type): 1.c4 Nf6 2.g3 e6 3.Bg2 Bb4+! 4.Nd2 O-O 5.Nf3 d5 — Bogo-Indian check disrupts White's smooth development. If 4.Bd2 Bxd2+ 5.Qxd2, Austin plays ...d5 and gets the same structure. Tarun has 0 Bogo-Indian experience in the dataset.
Tarun played 1...e5 then ...c6 as Black vs Liu (2153) in the English. This gives a reversed Sicilian King structure. Austin can try this as a surprise but should note that Tarun has experience on both sides of this structure.
Tarun: 1.c4 e5 2.g3 Nf6 3.Bg2 d5! 4.cxd5 Nxd5 — Reversed Sicilian with early ...d5 break. This equalizes immediately and leads to sharp symmetrical positions. Use only if Austin wants to surprise Tarun — it's a secondary option, not the main recommendation.

Recommendation: Default to Branch A (1...Nf6 2.Nc3 e6 → Mikenas-ready) or Branch B (Queen's Indian/Bogo). The Reversed Sicilian is a low-prep surprise option.
