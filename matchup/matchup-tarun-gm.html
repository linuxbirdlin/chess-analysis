Main move order — Rossolimo 4.e5! system

Austin: 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5! Nf6 4.e5! Nd5 5.Nc3! — 5.Nc3 scores 56%W (1,827 games). The key: Black's Nd5 is pinned against the Nc6; Nc3 attacks it again. Black must decide between 5...Nc7 (retreating), 5...Nxc3 (exchanging), 5...e6 (supporting), or 5...Nb4 (counterplay). All give White the advantage per Lichess data. White continues O-O, Re1, d4 — dominating the center with the e5 wedge.
After 5.Nc3: Nc7 6.O-O! e6 7.Re1! d6 8.exd6! Qxd6 9.d4 cxd4 10.Nxd4 — White recaptures with knight, landing on d4 — the ideal central square. Alternative after 6.O-O: Nxb5 — If Black captures the bishop (6...Nxb5? 7.Nxb5 e6 8.d4): White has a huge center pawn advantage for the sacrificed bishop. 7...cxd4?? 8.Nxd4 is even better for White. This line is rare but very good for Austin.

Strategic idea: Austin's Re1 supports the e5 pawn, d4 challenges the center, and the Nc3–Nd4 pair dominates. Black's pieces are awkward (Nc7 is passive, Nc6 is under pressure). White controls more space throughout.

Golden line: If Tarun plays 5...Nxc3, White scores 62%W — the highest White score in this entire opening cluster (547 games, Lichess 1800–2200). Black eliminates the c3-knight but gives White a half-open d-file and a strong center.
