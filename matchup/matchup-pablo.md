## Austin as White

### Critical challenge: Pablo plays Sicilian Kan (2...e6)

Austin's standard Rossolimo (3.Bb5) specifically requires **2...Nc6**. Pablo plays **2...e6** (Kan setup). After 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 **e6**, Austin's Rossolimo does NOT directly apply.

Austin needs to prepare for Open Sicilian vs Kan:

### Recommended: English Attack (B41/B43)

```
1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 e6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 Nf6 (or 4...a6)
5.Nc3 a6 6.Be3 d6 7.f3 b5 8.g4!
```

**Why this works:**
- Austin has practiced the English Attack structure in preparation vs Zachary, Pranav, and Emerson
- Pablo has NO documented games against 8.g4! English Attack as Black — this is new territory for him
- The English Attack shuts down Pablo's queenside counterplay (...b5, ...Rc8) before it starts
- Pablo understands the Kan from the Black side, but defending against White's g4 pawn storm is different from being the one playing it

**Move branches:**
- 8...Nfd7 (most common): 9.Qd2 Nc6 10.O-O-O b4 11.Nxc6 bxc3 12.bxc3 → White has kingside attack
- 8...Bb7: 9.g5 Nfd7 10.h4 → devastating kingside storm
- If Pablo plays 4...a6 (pure Kan): 5.Nc3 Qc7 6.Bd3 Nf6 7.O-O d6 8.f4 → similar structure

### Alternative: Alapin (2.c3)

```
1.e4 c5 2.c3
```

**Why consider it:**
- Sidesteps all Kan/Taimanov theory; Pablo must play ...d5 or ...Nf6
- After 2...d5 3.exd5 Qxd5 4.d4 → positions similar to Exchange Caro-Kann (Austin's territory)
- After 2...Nf6 3.e5 Nd5 4.Nf3 → positions Austin knows
- Risk: Pablo knows Alapin from the other side — he's played it himself as White (North American Blitz R9)
