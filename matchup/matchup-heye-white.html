§5 — Model Game / Reference Plan

The a4 challenge in the Kan (B43) was a key weapon of Kasparov and Anand in their 1990s B43/B47 battles. The structural plan:

  1. Development (moves 6–9): Be2 or Bf3, Re1 (or O-O), prepare a4. Do not let Black's b5 advance unchallenged.
  2. a4 challenge (move 9): Disrupt Black's b5 pawn before it becomes a queenside wedge. Force Heye to decide on b4, bxa4, or d6 — each concedes something.
  3. Nd5 outpost (moves 10–12): After b4 → Nd5 Qa5 Nxc6 Bxc6 Nxb4. The knight on d5 dominates the position. Black must either trade (giving doubled c-pawns) or suffer permanent pressure.
  4. Endgame alert: Ra7 technique — rook behind the passed pawn, NOT in front. This is the precise miss from the direct encounter (c8=Q+ was wrong; Ra7 wins cleanly).
Direct encounter lesson (Heye 93.9% · Austin 96%): Austin had a technically winning rook ending but played c8=Q+ instead of Ra7. This specific endgame pattern — rook + pawn vs rook, rook on the 7th — is the one technique to review before the game. Heye made 3 mistakes (d6 CPL 228, b4 CPL 191, Rxg2+ CPL 226) — his worst accuracy by color — confirming Black is his weaker side in this matchup.
