§5 — Model Game / Reference Plan

The Leningrad Dutch (A86) has been a weapon of GMs with aggressive styles who want unbalanced positions against systematic 1.Nf3/1.c4 players.

Structural plan (Leningrad):

  1. Development (moves 1–8): ...f5, ...Nf6, ...g6, ...Bg7, ...O-O, ...d6. The fianchettoed bishop on g7 fights against White's queenside expansion.
  2. Central plan (moves 9–14): ...Ne4 (the key piece — occupies the outpost), ...Nc6 (supports Ne4 and pressures d4), ...e5 (the central break when ready).
  3. Attack (moves 15+): If White plays passively, ...f4-f3 pawn storm. If White plays c5, break with ...e5! central counter. The dragon bishop on g7 activates on the long diagonal after ...e5 exd4.
  4. Endgame alert: Avoid trading the g7 bishop unless getting back the d4-pawn or significant compensation. The bishop is Austin's most valuable piece.
Key lesson from GV G267722: Zachary cracked when asymmetric queenside counterplay arrived in the Symmetrical English. The Leningrad Dutch creates asymmetry from move 1.
