§1 — Reyansh's White System

  • vs Sicilian 1...c5: Open Sicilian 2.Nf3 3.d4 always
  • vs Accelerated Dragon (...Nc6 + 4...g6): 5.c4 MARÓCZY BIND (8+ games)
  • vs Najdorf/Classical (...d6): 5.Nc3 standard
  • vs Taimanov/Kan (...e6): 5.Nc3 standard (NOT Maróczy)
  • vs 1...e5: Scotch 3.d4 (primary, 8/11 games)
  • vs Caro-Kann: Nc3 classical

The Maróczy Bind — Reyansh's Best System

  • Triggered by: 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 g6 5.c4!

  • His depth in this system far exceeds his other setups
  • White denies d5 outpost, controls c5, squeezes Black's space
  • Austin must NEVER reach this position

§2 — The Critical Decision: Taimanov vs Accelerated Dragon

NEVER play 2...Nc6 then 4...g6. If Austin plays 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 g6 — Reyansh plays 5.c4 MARÓCZY BIND. This is his most-practiced, best-prepared White system (8+ documented games). The Maróczy Bind structurally suffocates Black by denying the d5 square. Reyansh has demonstrated excellent handling of this structure against a range of opponents including IM-rated Petrov (2335). Do NOT go there.

§3 — Recommended Defense: Taimanov Sicilian (2...e6)

Play Taimanov: 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 e6. The 2...e6 move-order permanently prevents the Maróczy Bind. After 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 Nc6, Reyansh plays 5.Nc3 — his standard non-specialized response. Austin is in familiar Taimanov territory where Reyansh has no rehearsed deep plan. This is Austin's preparation advantage: Reyansh's depth drops significantly in 5.Nc3 e6 lines vs his 5.c4 Maróczy territory.

Main Line: Taimanov 5...Qc7 (B47 Bastrikov — Lichess master consensus)

Reyansh: 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 e6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 Nc6 5.Nc3 Qc7 — B47 Bastrikov variation. 5...Qc7 is the master main line (66% of games at master level). Protects the c5-square, prepares ...a6 and ...Nf6, avoids ...Nb5 tricks.
BRANCH A 6.Be3 a6 7.Bd3 Nf6 — English Attack (most common, 47%)
5...Qc7 6.Be3 a6 7.Bd3 Nf6 8.0-0 d6 9.f4 Be7

After 6.Be3 (Lichess masters: 12,104 games, 47% — most common White 6th move): White develops the bishop before castling, preparing f4-f5 kingside attack. Austin plays ...a6, ...Nf6, ...d6, ...Be7, then castles. Plan: ...b5 queenside expansion to counter f4-f5 pressure. Watch for f5 advance — Austin should prepare ...e5 or ...d5 to close/counter. If White plays f4-f5, Austin has ...e5 to close the center and transfer to queenside play with ...Nc6-e5 outpost.

BRANCH B 6.Be2 a6 7.0-0 Nf6 8.Be3 Be7 — Classical (26%)
5...Qc7 6.Be2 a6 7.0-0 Nf6 8.Be3 Be7 9.f4 d6 10.Qe1

After 5...Qc7 6.Be2 (Lichess masters: 6,613 games, 26%): Austin plays 6...a6 (now safe — Nb5 is prevented by Qc7) then develops: ...Nf6, ...d6, ...Be7, ...0-0. Plan: ...b5 queenside expansion, ...Bb7, ...Nc6-e5 knight outpost. Reyansh has no documented deep preparation in B47 — his 5.Nc3 games are mostly vs other Sicilian setups. The Bastrikov is Carlsen/Nepomniachtchi territory and a theoretically sound system.

BRANCH C 6.g3 — Fianchetto (Reyansh's likely alternative, 15%)
5...Qc7 6.g3 a6 7.Bg2 Nf6 8.0-0 Be7 9.a3 d6 → ...b5

After 6.g3 (Lichess masters: 3,857 games, 15%): Reyansh's Maróczy background (g3+Bg2 structure) makes this his most likely alternative to the English Attack. Austin plays 6...a6 (93% of masters), then 7.Bg2 Nf6 8.0-0 Be7 9.a3 d6. White scores 50.5% — roughly equal. Austin's plan is the same: ...b5-b4 queenside expansion, ...Bb7, knight to e5 or d4. Do not be surprised by 6.g3 — it is manageable and White holds no advantage. Austin should be comfortable: the e6 pawn gives more central control than in a true Dragon structure.

BRANCH D 6.g4 — Keres Attack (aggressive)
5...Qc7 6.g4 a6 7.g5 d5! 8.exd5 Nxd5

If Reyansh plays 6.g4 (Keres Attack against Taimanov): Austin continues 6...a6, then strikes back immediately with 7...d5! After 8.exd5 Nxd5 9.Nxd5 exd5, Black has active piece play and the d5-pawn as a lever. Reyansh has no Keres Attack games documented vs Taimanov — if he plays it, he is improvising. Austin should be comfortable with the dynamic counterplay.

DO NOT PLAY 5...a6 (B46 Kan) — NOT Austin's line

Austin's Black line is B47 Bastrikov (5...Qc7) ONLY. Never play 5...a6 (B46 Kan) as a backup. The Kan creates a specific trap: after 5...a6 6.Ndb5 Bc5?? 7.Bf4! O-O 8.Bc7! Qe7 9.Bd6 Bxd6 10.Qxd6 — bishop trap, game effectively over at move 10 (PAM 2026 R7 source). The B47 Bastrikov (5...Qc7) avoids this trap entirely and is Austin's documented, practiced system. If you have already played 5...Qc7, the game is in correct territory — stay on 5...Qc7.

TRAP The Maróczy Bind — what NOT to do
1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 g6 → 5.c4! (Maróczy!) DANGER

NEVER reach this position. If Austin plays 2...Nc6 and 4...g6, Reyansh plays 5.c4 — guaranteed. He has done this against Nalakonda (draw), Yenamandra (win), Petrov (draw vs 2335!), Yi Colin, Qi Jenny, Mokeeva, Natraj, Jin David — 8+ confirmed Maróczy games. The Maróczy Bind denies the d5 square permanently, squeezes Black's space, and Reyansh knows the plans deeply. His White accuracy (92.8%) is at its best in this system. 2...e6 is mandatory to prevent 5.c4.
