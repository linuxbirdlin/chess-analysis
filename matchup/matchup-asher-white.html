§3 — Recommended Weapon: Advanced French (3.e5)

Recommended: Advanced French 3.e5. Austin knows this system — it creates asymmetric pawn chains, avoids Asher's Tarrasch preparation, and suits Austin's tactical attacking style. Asher plays the standard ...c5 Nc6 response, which is well-known territory for both, but Austin's sharper attacking plans in the Advance give genuine winning chances.

Main Line: Advance French with 6.a3

Austin: 1.e4 e6 2.d4 d5 3.e5 c5 4.c3 Nc6 5.Nf3 Qb6 6.a3 — Prepares b4 to gain queenside space and prevent ...Nh6-f5. Sharp attacking play begins.
MAIN A 6…Nh6 7.b4 cxb4 8.axb4 Nxb4 9.Bd3 — most common sharp line
6.a3 Nh6 7.b4 cxb4 8.axb4 Nxb4 9.Bd3 Bd7 10.O-O Nc6 11.Ba3!

After 11.Ba3: White threatens Bb2 and Nc3 with powerful pressure. Black's Nb4 is unstable and must retreat. Austin gets open lines for attack and the bishop pair. This is sharp, concrete play that suits Austin's tactical strengths.

MAIN B 6.Be2 — classical Advance development
6.Be2 cxd4 7.cxd4 Nge7 8.Nc3 Nf5 9.Na4 Qd8 10.O-O Be7

After 6.Be2: White develops classically, maintaining the e5 pawn chain. Plan is f4-f5 kingside attack or c4 break to open center. Asher will likely play ...Nge7 ...Nf5 to pressure d4. Austin can meet with Na4 to drive back the queen or allow complications on the queenside.

SIDELINE After 3.e5 if Asher plays 3…Qb6 early (occasional French Advance sideline)
3.e5 Qb6 4.Nf3 Bd7 5.a3 Na5 6.Bd3 Bb5 7.Bxb5+ Nxb5 8.O-O

If Asher plays 3…Qb6: Austin proceeds with 4.Nf3 and a3 preparation for b4, not worrying about the queen on b6. After …Na5 and …Bb5, White can swap bishop and castle. Asher’s queen on b6 can become a target once White castles and opens the center.

§4 — Backup System: Exchange French (3.exd5)

Backup option: Exchange French sidesteps ALL of Asher's French preparation — Tarrasch, Rubinstein, Advance. The resulting symmetric position requires Austin to win through technique and piece activity. Use this if unsure of specific Advance lines or wanting a lower-complexity game.
