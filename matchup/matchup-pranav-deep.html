Pranav plays 1…e5 exclusively. Against Italian (3.Bc4): Giuoco Piano with 3…Bc5 4.c3 Nf6. Against Ruy Lopez (3.Bb5): classical Lopez with 3…a6 4.Ba4 Nf6 5…b5 6…Bc5 (Chigorin) or 6…Be7.

The common thread: White established a Nd5 outpost after the d4 break and Pranav's pieces gradually ran out of active ideas. His accuracy remained high, meaning these weren't tactical collapses — they were strategic grinding losses.

Recommended: Ruy Lopez with d4 plan

Ruy Lopez — d4 central plan (primary recommendation)

Key tactical themes (Austin as White)

  1. d4 central break: Play it when Black's Na5 is on the rim or when the c-file is open. This is the moment the game tips.
  2. Nd5 outpost: After d4, maneuver Nbd2-Nf1-Ng3-Nh5 or Nbd2-Nf1-Ne3-Nd5. Once the knight lands on d5, Pranav's queenside coordination collapses (seen in WY R4, R5, R7).
  3. Expect …g5 / …g4 over-extension: WY R4 (vs Gabadadze) showed Pranav playing …g5-g4 when under positional pressure. Answer with d5! central advance, not passive defense.
  4. Do NOT play quiet Italian (Bc4+d3): Pranav scored 2W–0L as White in Italian positions at World Youth — he is deep in Italian theory as both colors. Avoid his preparation.

Recommended: Taimanov with 6…Qc7 vs English Attack

Taimanov vs English Attack

1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 e6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 Nc6 5.Nc3 a6
6.Be3 Qc7   ← prevents Nd5 (queen covers d6)
7.Bd3 Nf6 8.Qe2 d6 9.O-O-O Be7 10.g4

6…Qc7 prevents the immediate Nd5 trick. Then develop solidly with …Nf6/…d6/…Be7. After White plays g4, respond with …h6 to slow the storm, then generate queenside counterplay with …b5/…Rb8.

Anti-English Attack principles

  1. 6…Qc7 is non-negotiable: Without it, 7.Nd5! immediately creates problems. The Qc7 move covers d6 and keeps options open.
  2. Survive to move 25: Engine data shows Pranav's English Attack can lose steam around move 25 (WY R8: g3 blunder). Austin's goal in the first 24 moves is not to win — it's to avoid catastrophe and reach a middlegame where Pranav needs to calculate precisely.
  3. Counter f4-f5 with …e5: If Pranav launches f3-f4-f5 storm, …e5! closes the file. Have this response ready as a reflex.
  4. Queenside race (...Rb8 / ...b5 / ...b4): Against White's O-O-O, this is Black's life. Start the counterattack early — every tempo counts.
WY R10 (Pranav vs Ragavan, Taimanov, 0-1): Pranav used the Nxc6 exchange system and won material but couldn't convert a 73-move endgame at 93.8% accuracy. This confirms: against Taimanov, even when Pranav "wins" the opening battle, his endgame with the Taimanov structures is not reliable.

Key tactical themes (Austin as Black)

  1. Survive to move 25: Pranav's English Attack peaks in the 15–25 move range. If Austin is still in the game at move 25, Pranav may become passive (as in WY R8).
  2. …e5 vs f4-f5: Central counter to the pawn storm. Non-negotiable to know this.
  3. Clock psychology: AGCA R9 shows Pranav panics when he encounters unfamiliar territory. If Austin plays a relatively rare Taimanov line (e.g. early …d5 break or unusual …Nd4 ideas), Pranav may spend 10–15 minutes trying to figure it out.

This is a double-edged trait: Austin won't get a free draw, but Pranav will sometimes over-press in worse positions (WY R4: h4 pawn advance at –0.77 that dropped to –5.93 immediately).

Core preparation summary:
  • White: Ruy Lopez → d4 break → Nd5 outpost. Be patient. Pranav's Lopez as Black is solid but strategically weak once the Nd5 is established. Pranav's 3 Lopez/Italian Black losses at WY prove this.

  • Clock: Play opening moves confidently. Let Pranav think. He burns time in unfamiliar positions.
  • Don't over-press: At ~equal ratings, a draw is a real result. Don't sacrifice material desperately trying to create chaos — Pranav's 93% WY accuracy means he'll punish tactical mistakes reliably.
