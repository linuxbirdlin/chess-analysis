Part 1 — Austin as White: English Attack Targets Unprepared Kan

The core challenge: Pablo's primary Black weapon is the Sicilian Kan (2…e6 4…a6). Austin's normal Rossolimo (3.Bb5) requires 2…Nc6 — Pablo doesn't play 2…Nc6. Austin must face the Open Sicilian. The weapon: English Attack (6.Be3 7.f3 8.g4!) — a concrete pawn storm that Pablo has zero documented games defending against.

English Attack — Austin's main line

Austin: 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 e6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 Nf6 5.Nc3 a6 6.Be3 d6 7.f3 b5 8.g4! — The English Attack pawn storm. 8.g4 prevents …Nf6 development, stops …b4 queenside expansion, and launches a direct kingside attack. Pablo has zero documented games defending against this — he must improvise from move 8 in unfamiliar territory.
Austin: 8.g4 Nfd7 9.Qd2 Nc6 10.O-O-O b4 11.Nxc6! bxc3 12.bxc3 → — Open b-file for Austin + mobile center. After 12.bxc3, plan h4-h5-g5-h6 attack. Pablo's queenside counterplay is neutralized. Austin's mobile center (f3/g4/h4) creates decisive threats.

Key plan after 12.bxc3: play 13.h4 threatening h5. If Pablo tries …Bc5 to attack the f3 pawn, answer with 14.Kb1 O-O 15.Bd3 and a4 to fix his queenside. The attack rolls naturally.

Austin: 8.g4 Bb7 9.g5! Nfd7 10.h4 Nc6 11.Qd2 Nxd4 12.Bxd4 → — 9.g5! immediately advances the pawn storm. After 10.h4, Black has no counterplay — the kingside attack is unstoppable. White plans h5-h6 or g6-h6-Qh7. Pablo's Bb7 is shut out of the game.

This is the sharpest variation. Austin should know this to move 15. After 12.Bxd4, White has full center, pawn storm, and Black's position is cramped. Continue with 13.Qe3 preparing f4-f5.

Austin: 4.Nxd4 a6 5.Nc3 Qc7 6.Bd3! Nf6 7.O-O d6 8.f4! Nc6 9.Nxc6 bxc6 10.e5! — vs the pure Kan (4…a6), Austin uses the f4 system. After 10.e5!, Black's position is cramped and the knight must retreat. Continue with 11.Be3, 12.Qe1 (preparing Qh4) attack.

The f4 system vs pure Kan is well-theoretical. Austin's advantage: he's prepared attacking setups from other matchups. Pablo would need to know the Kan vs f4 system specifically — not his usual territory.

Alternative: 1.e4 c5 2.c3 sidesteps all Kan theory. Pablo plays Alapin himself sometimes so he knows one side of it. But as Black vs Alapin, he may be less practiced. After 2…d5 3.exd5 Qxd5, the queen is a target and White builds central pressure.
Austin: 2.c3 d5 3.exd5 Qxd5 4.d4 Nf6 5.Nf3 e6 6.Bd3! — After 6.Bd3 Nc6 7.O-O Be7 8.c4! Qd8 9.Nc3, White has strong center. Austin knows d4/Bd3 from Exchange Caro-Kann. Pablo is out of Kan preparation from move 2.

Part 2 — Austin as Black: Taimanov Mirror vs English Attack

Core insight: Austin plays 1…c5. Pablo uses the English Attack (f3/Be3/Qd2/g4) vs Dragon/Sicilian. Austin should use the Taimanov/Kan (4…e6 5.Nc3 a6) — the SAME structure Pablo plays as Black. Austin has studied this from both sides; this is his most-drilled defensive structure of 2026. The key: trade the Nd4 EARLY (8…Nxd4!) before Pablo plays 9.g4!

Austin's Taimanov — main defense

Pablo: 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 e6 5.Nc3 a6 6.Be3 Nge7 7.f3 d6 8.Qd2 Nxd4! — CRITICAL defensive move. Trading the Nd4 BEFORE Pablo plays 9.g4 defuses the English Attack completely. After 9.Bxd4 Nc6 10.Be3, Austin has a solid B48 Taimanov with no kingside attack coming. This is Austin's most-practiced defensive technique of 2026.
Austin: 8…Nxd4! 9.Bxd4 Nc6 10.Be3 Be7 11.O-O-O O-O 12.Kb1 b5 — Solid Taimanov B48. White has no g4 attack (no Nd4 to support it). Austin develops normally: …Be7, …O-O, then generates queenside counterplay with …b5/…Bb7/…d5. Pablo must find a plan from an unfamiliar position where his attack has been defused.

Austin has drilled this from the White side vs Zachary's Taimanov (B46). He knows exactly where Black's counterplay lies (…b4, …Rc8, …d5 breaks) and how White's piece coordination works. This is Austin's territory.

Pablo: 6.Be3 Nge7 7.Be2 (quieter) d6 8.O-O Nxd4 9.Bxd4 Nc6 — If Pablo plays Be2 instead of f3+Qd2, the attack never materializes. Austin's …Nxd4 trade still works to simplify. Continue with standard Kan development. Austin is comfortable in this positional territory.
Pablo: 1.e4 c5 2.c3 d5! 3.exd5 Qxd5 4.d4 Nf6 5.Nf3 e6 6.Bd3 Nc6 — Central break. After 2…d5!, the queen goes to d5 and gets kicked, but Austin reaches a solid position. White must develop naturally while Austin generates queenside play. Austin knows Exchange Caro-Kann-type central structures (4.Bd3 plans). Avoid 2…e6 which can transpose to French.
Pablo: 1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 e6 3.Nc3 Bb4! 4.Qc2 O-O 5.a3 Bxc3+! 6.Qxc3 b6 — Nimzo-Indian Classical. Austin's standard Bxc3+ …b6/…Ba6 system. Pablo has played some 1.d4 games (American Continental Blitz R4 vs Nimzo opponent) — he knows the general ideas but Austin has deeper Nimzo preparation.
