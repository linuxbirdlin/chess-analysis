Part 1 — Austin as White: Rossolimo Sidesteps Sveshnikov

Primary weapon: Rossolimo (3.Bb5!). Yaen's Black weapon is the Sveshnikov (B32/B33) — requiring 3.d4 → 4.Nxd4 → 4...e5. By playing 3.Bb5 instead of 3.d4, Austin bypasses every line Yaen has prepared. Yaen has zero documented games as Black against the Rossolimo. He must improvise from move 3.

Rossolimo — Austin's main line

Austin: 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5! — The Sveshnikov requires 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 e5. 3.Bb5 cuts the entire Sveshnikov tree dead. Yaen is in unfamiliar territory from move 3.
Austin: 3.Bb5 g6 4.Bxc6! dxc6 5.d3 Bg7 6.h3 Nf6 7.Nc3! O-O 8.Be3 b6 9.Qd2! — Exchange Rossolimo. After 4.Bxc6 dxc6, Yaen has doubled c-pawns and a structural deficit. White builds a kingside pawn center. 9.Qd2 prepares Bh6 to trade Black's fianchettoed bishop, killing the long diagonal.

Key plan: Bh6 to remove the Bg7, then f4–f5 kingside expansion. Yaen's doubled c-pawns are a permanent structural weakness — target c5 in all endgames.

Austin: 3.Bb5 e6 4.O-O Nge7 5.c3! a6 6.Ba4 b5 7.Bc2 d5 8.d4! — Classical Rossolimo with c3/Ba4. The 8.d4! central break creates immediate tension. Yaen has never faced this from Black; he will either trade into a slightly inferior IQP structure or allow White a strong center.
Austin: 3.Bb5 Nf6 4.Bxc6! dxc6 5.e5! Nd5 6.O-O Nb6 7.d3! Bg4 8.Nc3 e6 9.h3! — White has the e5 pawn wedge and central space. After 5.e5 Nd5 6.O-O, White controls the entire center. 9.h3 kicks Bg4 before Black can create pressure with ...Bh5–Bg6.

Austin should use this if Yaen tries to quickly develop with 3...Nf6 — the e5 pawn is a powerful space advantage White maintains throughout the game.

Austin plays 1...c5 (Sicilian) as Black, so in a normal game Austin is Black and Yaen is White. However if Austin unexpectedly plays 1...e5, Yaen would use his Scotch. This branch is for reference only — it won't arise unless Austin deviates. Yaen's Scotch (C45) is his primary weapon as White, but Austin's Sicilian avoids it entirely.
If Austin plays 1...e5 (not recommended): 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.d4 (Scotch) — Yaen's most prepared territory. Avoid: play 1...c5 to sidestep Scotch entirely.

Part 2 — Austin as Black: Taimanov/Kan vs B90 English Attack

Core fact: Austin plays 1...c5. The Scotch Game (Yaen's primary White weapon) requires 1...e5. Since Austin plays Sicilian, Yaen switches to his B90 English Attack system (f3/Be3/Qd2/g4). Austin should use the Taimanov/Kan (4...e6 5.Nc3 a6) — familiar ground from Austin's Pranav, Heye, and Emerson prep — where he has a preparation advantage.

Austin's Taimanov/Kan — main line

Yaen: 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 e6 5.Nc3 a6 6.Be3 Nge7 7.f3 d6 8...Nxd4! — Taimanov/Kan B48 structure. Austin plays ...e6+...a6 and develops the knight to e7 (not f6) to keep flexibility. 8...Nxd4 and recapturing 9.Bxd4 avoids the English Attack's sharpest lines. Austin knows this structure deeply.
Austin: 4...e6 5.Nc3 a6 6.Be3 Nge7 7.f3 d6 8.Qd2 Nxd4! 9.Bxd4 Nc6 10...Be7! — Taimanov B48. 8...Nxd4! reduces tension before Yaen can launch the g4-g5 attack. After 9.Bxd4 Nc6 10.Be3 Be7, Black has a solid position. Yaen's English Attack preparation (g4/h4 attack) is blunted before it starts.

This is well-trodden territory for Austin (familiar from Pranav, Heye, Emerson analysis). The key is trading the Nd4 before Yaen can play 9.g4! — once White plays g4, the attack is on.

Surprise option: Play 4...e5 (Kalashnikov/Sveshnikov from the Black side). Yaen knows this structure deeply as Black — but as White, he has zero documented anti-Sveshnikov/anti-Kalashnikov games. He will be in his own territory but on the wrong side of the board.
Austin: 4.Nxd4 e5! 5.Nb5 d6 6.N1c3 a6 7.Na3 b5 8.Nd5! Nce7! — Kalashnikov B32/Sveshnikov B33. Yaen plays this EXACT structure as Black — his B32/B33 games show deep knowledge. As White, he has never faced it. Austin plays his own opening back at him with the roles reversed.

Risk: Austin needs to know the Sveshnikov/Kalashnikov from the Black side (which he should now study, since it mirrors his prep). Reward: Yaen will be in unfamiliar territory as White in his own opening — the same territory where he made three consecutive blunders as Black (Olimpiada Rapid R5).

Yaen: 1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 e6 3.Nc3 Bb4! 4.Qc2 O-O 5.a3 Bxc3+! 6.Qxc3 b6 — Nimzo-Indian Classical. Austin's standard Bxc3+ ...b6/...Ba6 system. Yaen has played D00/London and QGD as Black vs 1.d4 — he has not been observed handling Nimzo-Indian as White. Austin has deep Nimzo preparation.

If Yaen plays 1.d4 London (D00 — his recent 2026 games), Austin plays Nimzo-Indian and outprepares him in a system Yaen hasn't faced much as White.
