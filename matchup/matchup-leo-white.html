Why Advanced French specifically:

  • Leo plays 3.e5 c5 in Advance French — the resulting positions have exactly the type of knight maneuvering (Nc6, Nb4, knight repositioning) where Leo's CPL-456/440 collapses occur.
  • Austin's repertoire includes the Advance French as a main system. This is familiar tactical territory for Austin, unexplored-at-collapse-level for Leo.
  • The Advance French does NOT simplify to a technical grind early — it maintains pawn tension (e5 vs ...c5) through the middlegame, exactly what Austin needs.

Alternative: Tarrasch 3.Nd2

1.e4 e6 2.d4 d5 3.Nd2 Nf6 4.e5 Nfd7 5.Bd3 c5 6.c3 Nc6 7.Ne2 → IQP structure; knight on d2 blocks c3-pawn's scope; Austin targets f4 break

The Tarrasch leads to IQP structures where the knight on d7 (Leo's typical setup) often needs precise coordination — again Leo's weakness area. Slightly slower than the Advance, but equally valid.

Do NOT play Exchange French 3.exd5. Leo won in 134 moves in this structure (NA U20 R8 vs Cruz Vargas). The Exchange French simplifies immediately and gives Leo the technical endgame he excels in. Austin should avoid the symmetric pawn structure entirely.
