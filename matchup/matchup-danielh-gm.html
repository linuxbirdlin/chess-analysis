Part 1 — Austin as Black: Taimanov Sicilian B47 Bastrikov (PRIMARY)

Austin's standard Taimanov — mandatory move order

Daniel: 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 e6! 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 Nc6 5.Nc3 Qc7! — B47 Bastrikov. 5...Qc7 is mandatory — not 5...a6 (Kan), not 5...Nf6 (B45). The queen on c7 controls the c-file, prepares ...a6+...d6+...Nf6 structure, and avoids the Ndb5 bishop trap (see Branch D below).
Daniel: 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 e6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 Nc6 5.Nc3 Qc7 6.Be3 a6 7.Bd3 Nf6 8.O-O d6 9.f4 Be7 10.Qf3 b5! — English Attack. Austin's response: ...a6 immediately (secures the queenside knight's deployment), then ...Nf6, ...d6, ...Be7. After 10.Qf3 or 10.Kh1, play ...b5! — the standard Taimanov queenside break. Austin has prepared this structure from multiple matchup files.

Plan after 10...b5!: ...Bb7 or ...Rb8, ...Nxd4 (exchanging one knight to relieve pressure), then ...b4 to push White's Nc3 back. This is Austin's home territory — PAM 2026 R4 (draw vs 2069) used this exact structure. Austin outprepares Daniel here.

Key decision point: If Daniel plays 9.Nxc6 bxc6 10.e5, Austin plays 10...Nd7! (not 10...Ng4?). After 10...Nd7 11.exd6 Bxd6 Black has the two bishops and active counterplay.
Daniel: 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 e6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 Nc6 5.Nc3 Qc7 6.Be2 a6 7.O-O Nf6 8.Be3 Be7 9.f4 d6 10.Kh1 O-O — Classical Be2. Same pattern as Branch A but White's bishop is on e2 not d3 (less aggressive; Austin has more time). After ...O-O, play ...b5 queenside break or ...Nxd4 Bxd4 to simplify the position.

With Be2 instead of Bd3, White lacks the f4-f5 pawn storm momentum. Austin should steer toward an IQP position or queenside counterplay with ...b5-b4. Daniel's handling of the Taimanov as White (B44 game in dataset) was not remarkable — Austin has the deeper preparation here.

Simplification option: After O-O and Be3, play ...Nxd4 Bxd4 to exchange one pair of knights. This reduces tactical density and steers to a position where Austin's endgame preparation advantage over Daniel shows.
Daniel: 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 e6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 Nc6 5.Nc3 Qc7 6.g3 a6 7.Bg2 Nf6 8.O-O Be7 9.Be3 d6 10.f4 O-O — Fianchetto system. White's Bg2 controls d5 but lacks the attacking energy of Be3+f4. Fianchetto is less common at Daniel's level and signals less preparation. Austin should stay in the main Taimanov structure — no need to deviate. The b5-b4 queenside push is reliable here as White's pieces are aimed kingside.
