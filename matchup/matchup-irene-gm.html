Primary Sparring Objectives (derived from deep analysis)

Objective 1 — Endgame conversion from +4 (Pattern D)

  • The finding: Irene drew four games from positions of +4.85 to +10.15 — opponents had completely winning positions but failed to convert. Austin should have the same ability as those opponents to squander a won game, and the same ability to not squander it.
  • Practice objective: When Austin is ≥+4 as White (Tarrasch endgame), set a rule: no simplification until a decisive material advantage is guaranteed. Calculate each trade; don't rely on "I'm clearly winning."
  • Drill: Take positions from the Valdizon draw (Rf7/Rxe6, +10.15) and Butenko draw (Qb3/d4, +4.85) — load them in Lichess analysis. Find the winning move. Practice the plan from those positions against Stockfish.
  • Success criterion: Austin converts every ≥+4 endgame in sparring without letting it slip to +1 or below.

Objective 2 — Pawn advance vs piece move in equal positions (Pattern G)

  • The finding: Two of Irene's White losses came from playing R/Q when a pawn push was the engine's correct plan: Qd2 when a5! (+1.69→+0.05), Rf5 when g3! (0.00→−5.55).
  • Austin's training target: This is a mirror drill. Austin should learn to RECOGNIZE the moment when a pawn break is available in an equal position. Before every R/Q move in any position between ±1.5, pause and ask: "Is there a pawn push here?"
  • As White (Tarrasch): The f5! advance or d5 break in equal middlegames. Don't improve pieces when the position calls for action.
  • As Black (Rossolimo): The ...b5!, ...c4!, or ...a4! breaks. Don't reposition knights when pawns should march.
  • Success criterion: Austin identifies pawn break opportunities before piece moves in every sparring game, whether or not the pawn move is ultimately correct.

Objective 3 — French Tarrasch preparation quality (Austin as White)

  • Context: NAYCC 2026 has several French-playing opponents (Asher Chodniewicz plays French all systems; Inti plays French Rubinstein C10). Austin's French prep needs to be sharp at tournament quality.
  • Practice objective: Reach move 15 with ≥97% accuracy in Tarrasch lines. Test both the 4.exd5 exd5 structure (preferred) and the Advance 3.e5 f4 storm.
  • Warning: Irene knows the Tarrasch-Chistyakov (3...c5 4.exd5 Qxd5). After the Qxd5 recapture, she has 3+ games at 92–95% accuracy. Deviate with 4...exd5 to get out of her preparation.
  • Success criterion: Reach post-book positions (move 15+) with ≥+0.5 advantage consistently.

Objective 4 — Taimanov/Rossolimo defense reps (Austin as Black)

  • Context: Irene's Rossolimo Exchange (4.Bxc6) is exactly the structure Austin faces from Emerson Johnson and Reyansh Paragiri. These sparring games are the most relevant Black prep available.
  • Practice objective: Master the doubled c-pawn structure after 4.Bxc6 bxc6 (b-pawn recapture — open b-file). Play ...Qc7, ...d6, ...Nf6, ...Be7, then activate the bishop pair via ...Bb7 (long diagonal) and ...Ba6 (exchange for Nf3 to open the e-file). Push ...b5! to use the open b-file. Never exchange bishops for knights — the bishop pair vs Irene's knights is Black's entire compensation.
  • Success criterion: Austin achieves bishop pair activity in every game — no passive exchanges that neutralize the compensation.
