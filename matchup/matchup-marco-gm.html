Part 1 — Austin as White: Rossolimo/Moscow vs Sicilian, Italian vs 1...e5

The core challenge: Marco plays Sicilian 1...c5 2...Nc6 as Black — directly entering Austin's Rossolimo 3.Bb5 weapon. His best games as Black (G9/G10, both 97%+) show he is fully comfortable in Sicilian counterplay, so Austin must not play for a closed positional squeeze. Instead: Exchange Rossolimo (4.Bxc6+ bxc6) to open the c-file, saddle Marco with doubled c-pawns, and generate a kingside attack. Marco won his two Exchange CK games as White, so he understands the structure — but as Black he has never faced it, and his conversion weakness emerges in complex piece activity games past move 25.

Branch A — Rossolimo (1...c5 2...Nc6, most likely)

After 4.Bxc6+ bxc6, Marco has doubled c-pawns and a half-open d-file. Austin's plan: castle queenside or keep the king flexible, deploy Be3–Qd2, and launch h4-h5 on the kingside. Marco's G7 Kd3 endgame pattern shows he navigates king-side piece activity poorly once pieces become active. The position should not simplify to a pure K+R ending — keep at least one minor piece on the board to exploit his conversion weakness.
