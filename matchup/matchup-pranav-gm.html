Part 1 — Austin as White vs Pranav

The core fact: Pranav plays 1…e5 in 100% of games vs 1.e4. His Giuoco Piano record as Black (3…Bc5 after 3.Bc4) is 0W-2D-1L — his weakest opening. Austin plays the Italian with 4.c3! 5.d4 — a sharp center-opening attack targeting exactly this weakness. This is also Austin's most natural style: central pawn play, IQP positions, knight outposts.

Pranav's Black repertoire vs 1.e4 (confirmed)

  • 1…e5 → 2…Nc6 always — confirmed in all 6 Black vs 1.e4 games
  • vs 3.Bc4: Giuoco Piano — 3…Bc5 (then …Nf6, …d6, …O-O)
  • vs 3.Bb5: Classical Lopez — 3…a6 4…Nf6 5…b5 6…Bc5 (not Berlin, not Marshall)
  • vs 3.d4 (Scotch): 3…exd4 → Mieses variation (WY R9 vs Ivancic — his only Black win vs 2100+)

Austin's weapon: Italian with 4.c3! 5.d4

The Evans Attack / Aggressive Italian: after 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4 Bc5 4.c3!, Austin prepares the d4 center break. Pranav plays …Nf6 or …Qe7 in this structure. After 5.d4 exd4 6.cxd4 Bb6 (his usual retreat), White has a powerful IQP + development advantage. Austin's IQP expertise from the Nimzo-Indian as Black transfers directly here.
Austin: 1.e4 e5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bc4 Bc5 4.c3! Nf6 5.d4! exd4 6.cxd4 Bb6 7.Nc3 d6 8.O-O O-O 9.h3! — h3 prevents …Bg4 pin; White has ideal IQP structure.
Austin: 4.c3 Nf6 5.d4 exd4 6.cxd4 Bb6 7.Nc3 d6 8.O-O O-O 9.h3 h6 10.Re1 Re8 11.d5! — closing the center with d5 gives White a permanent kingside bind. The IQP becomes a passed pawn on d5.

After 11.d5 Ne7 12.Nd4: White's knight dominates on d4. Plan is Bf4 + Qd2 + Rad1, doubling on the d-file. Pranav's bishop on b6 is completely passive.

If 8…Na5?! (chasing the bishop): 9.Bd3! — let him trade, keep the IQP structure. After 9…Nxd3? 10.Qxd3 d6 11.O-O Re8, White still has the central bind.

Austin: 4.c3 Qe7 5.O-O! Nf6 6.d4 exd4 7.cxd4 Nxe4 8.d5! Nd6 9.Re1 Qf8 10.Nc3 — Black's queen is misplaced on f8; White has strong central play and the d5 outpost.

After 10.Nc3: White has a pleasant advantage. Pranav's Nd6 is awkward, and the f7 square becomes a target after Ng5 ideas.

DO NOT play 3.Bb5 — steer toward 3.Bc4 Italian. Pranav has better results in Lopez (1W-0D-2L is still only 1 win, but it's his more familiar territory). Stay in the Italian where he's 0W-2D-1L.
Austin recommendation: Always play 3.Bc4 (not 3.Bb5) to reach Italian. If Pranav plays 3…Bc5 → Branch A/B. If Pranav plays 3…Nf6?! (Two Knights) → Austin plays 4.Ng5! targeting f7.

If 3…d6: This is the Philidor-adjacent Italian. 4.c3 Nf6 5.d4 exd4 6.cxd4 — same IQP structure; Pranav's bishop on e7/f8 area will be passive.

If 3…Nf6 (Two Knights): 4.Ng5! — the Italian Two Knights Attack. After 4.Ng5 d5 5.exd5 Na5 6.Bb5+ c6 7.dxc6 bxc6 8.Be2 h6 9.Nf3 e4, Austin has a sharp tactical position that suits his style.

Part 2 — Austin as Black vs Pranav

Core fact: Pranav plays 1.e4 → English Attack (Be3/f3/Qd2/O-O-O) vs Sicilian. Austin plays 1.e4 himself and runs the English Attack as White — he knows this system from both sides. Austin plays Sicilian Kan (1…c5, 2…e6, 3…a6). Pranav's English Attack record in the reliable dataset is only 1W-1D-1L (3 games). Austin should be MORE comfortable in this structure than Pranav.

Pranav's White repertoire vs Sicilian (confirmed)

  • vs Najdorf/Scheveningen (…d6+Nf6): English Attack — 6.Be3 then f3, Qd2, O-O-O, g4/h4
  • vs Accelerated Dragon (…Nc6+g6): Maróczy Bind — 5.c4 (better results: 2W-1D-1L)
  • vs Kan (…e6+a6): likely English Attack (same as Najdorf); possible Maróczy
  • Pranav is more dangerous with the Maróczy; steer him toward English Attack by playing Kan (not …g6)

Austin's weapon: Sicilian Kan → English Attack mirror

Austin plays the Kan and forces Pranav into the English Attack — a system Austin has used himself (he knows the ideas, the pawn breaks, the king-hunting patterns). Pranav has fewer prepared novelties in the English Attack than the Maróczy, and his English Attack record is only 1W-1D-1L.
Pranav: 1.e4 c5! 2.Nf3 e6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 a6 (Kan) 5.Nc3 Qc7 6.Be3 Nf6 7.f3 b5! 8.g4 h6! 9.Qd2 Bb7 10.O-O-O Nbd7 — standard Kan English Attack. …b5! launches queenside counterplay before g5 arrives.
After 10…Nbd7: Play 11…Rc8! — rook activates queenside. Then 12…b4! pushing queenside before g5 arrives. After 13.Na4: Qb8! (defends b4, keeps …Nc5 option) 14.Nb6 Nxb6 15.Qxb4 Nc4! — knight outpost on c4 is powerful; Austin wins the queenside race.

Critical rule: Do NOT play …d5?! — this gives Pranav the IQP structure he's comfortable with from his White games. Keep position dynamic with …b4 and …Nc5 queenside pressure.

When Pranav plays g5 (kingside storm): counter with …Ng8-e7-f5! rerouting the knight before the storm lands.

If 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 e6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 a6 5.c4 (Maróczy): Pranav has 2W-1D-1L with this system — more dangerous for Austin.

Pranav: 5.c4 Nf6 6.Nc3 Bb4! (Taimanov/Nimzo transposition) 7.Nxc6 bxc6! 8.Bd3 e5! — Black grabs the center immediately. The bishops become powerful on the long diagonal.

After 8…e5: White cannot play d5 without pawn structure damage. Black gets a solid hold on the center with …d6 and …Be6. This is analogous to Austin's Nimzo-Indian repertoire — he knows this type of position.

Pranav plays 1.e4 in 100% of White games in the dataset — 1.d4 is very unlikely. But if he does:

Pranav: 1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 e6 3.Nc3 Bb4 — Austin's Nimzo-Indian (his main Black weapon vs 1.d4). Pranav has no 1.d4 preparation in the dataset.
