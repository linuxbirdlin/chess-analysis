§3 — Austin's Defense: Solid QGD vs Nimzo-Larsen

1
Play 1...d5 immediately — claim the center
After 1.Nf3, play 1...d5. This controls the center immediately and stops the Nimzo-Larsen's slow buildup from gaining momentum. Never play 1...e5 — tactical tricks after 2.Nxe5 are possible.
2
After 2.b3 Nf6 3.Bb2: play 3...e6 4.e3 Be7 — QGD-type solid
Don't react to the long diagonal. Solid development: e6, Be7, O-O. The Nimzo-Larsen is not immediately threatening with correct play — it becomes dangerous only if Black over-reacts.
3
The critical break: 5.d4 O-O 6.Bd3 c5! — MANDATORY
...c5 must be played. Without it, Austin drifts into a passive hedgehog position where Joel's bishops gradually dominate. The ...c5 break challenges the d4 pawn and creates immediate counterplay. Austin's Nimzo-Indian background (similar pawn structures) provides the positional understanding needed here.
4
After 7.O-O Nc6 8.Nbd2 b6 — queenside expansion
The b6 move prepares ...Ba6, fighting the long diagonal head-on or creating queenside counterplay. Nc6 supports the d5/c5 structure. Austin has solid, coordinated pieces — time is on his side.
5
If Joel plays 4.g3 instead of 4.e3: play 4...c5! immediately
The g3 fianchetto variation means Joel is building toward a Bg2 setup. Counter immediately with 4...c5 (Benoni-type) before Joel can establish the fianchettoed bishop structure. Active counterplay beats passive waiting.
