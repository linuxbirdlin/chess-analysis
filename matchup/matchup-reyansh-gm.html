Part 1 (Secondary — Austin as White): Rossolimo Sicilian (3.Bb5)

Core challenge: Reyansh is deeply prepared in the Accelerated Dragon (5.c4 Maróczy Bind as White means he knows the pawn structures from both sides). Playing 3.d4 Open Sicilian gives him 4...g6 into familiar Accelerated Dragon/Maróczy territory. The Rossolimo (3.Bb5) cuts his preparation off immediately at move 3.
Austin: 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5 g6 4.0-0 Bg7 5.c3 Nf6 6.Re1 0-0 7.d4 cxd4 8.cxd4 d5 9.e5 Ne4 10.Nc3!

Position assessment: Anti-Rossolimo main line (Lichess masters: 77% of 4,165 games at the 5.c3 position). After 5...Nf6 Austin plays 6.Re1, preparing d4. The critical position is 9.e5 Ne4 — Austin plays 10.Nc3! (264 master games, 54.4% — main line). Nc3 attacks the Ne4 immediately. Austin must know the main continuation cold:

Win-attempt alternative: 10.Bxc6!? (122 master games, 62.3%) captures and forces 10...bxc6, creating permanent doubled c-pawns. Then 11.Ne5 targeting c6 with strong structural pressure. Use 10.Nc3 as the standard plan; 10.Bxc6 if you want maximum winning chances.

  • 10...Nxc3 11.bxc3 Na5 12.Bd3 Nc4 13.Bxc4 dxc4 14.d5! — the passed d-pawn is powerful and Austin has the initiative. This is the IQP tabiya Austin knows from Nimzo training. White's c3+d5 pawn structure gives a long-term space advantage.
  • Practical: after 12.Bd3, masters play 12...Bg4 (64%) more than 12...Nc4. If Bg4: 13.h3 Bxf3 14.Qxf3 — d4-d5 plan still follows.

Critical prep: The 9.e5 Ne4 10.Nc3! position is the most likely actual game position — know the IQP continuation before the game.

Austin: 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5 g6 4.0-0 Bg7 5.c3 e5 6.d3 Nge7 7.Bd2 0-0 8.Na3 d6 9.Nc2

If Reyansh closes the center with 5...e5, Austin plays d3 (solid) and maneuvers Na3-c2-e3 to occupy d5. The f4 advance is a key plan — after preparation, f4 strikes at e5 and opens the f-file. Black's Bg7 is strong defensively, but the closed pawn structure neutralizes some of its diagonal power. Austin aims for a slow strategic game where piece maneuvering dominates.

Austin: 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5 g6 4.Bxc6 dxc6 5.d3 Nf6 6.Nc3 Bg7 7.h3

After 4.Bxc6: Austin gives up bishop pair in exchange for permanently doubled c-pawns. Black has more space and the bishop pair, but the c4-square becomes a permanent White outpost for a knight. Austin targets: Ne2-d4 to dominate the c-file, or Ne2-g3 for kingside play. The structural edge compounds in endgames. Use when wanting maximum clarity, but note that Reyansh may be more comfortable in this technical endgame style — his Mechanics Institute 5W-0L record suggests positional strength.

Part 2 — ROUND 5 PRIORITY: Austin as Black — Taimanov B47 Bastrikov

Core insight: 2...e6 permanently prevents Reyansh's Maróczy Bind (5.c4, 8+ documented games, 94–97% accuracy). After 5...Qc7 (B47 Bastrikov — 66% of masters), Reyansh faces territory with less depth than his Maróczy home turf. Austin's comfortable variance: 6.Be3 a6 7.Bd3 Nf6 8.f4 Be7! — Lichess 1800–2200 shows White scoring only 33% here. Standard plan: ...0-0, then ...b5-b4 queenside counterplay.
Reyansh: 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 e6! 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 Nc6 5.Nc3 Qc7 — B47 Bastrikov (66% of 35,297 Lichess master games). 2...e6 kills Maróczy forever.
Reyansh: 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 e6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 Nc6 5.Nc3 Qc7 6.Be3 a6 7.Bd3 Nf6 8.f4 Be7! ← Austin's comfortable variance. White scores only 33% here (Lichess 1800–2200, 55 games)

Why 8...Be7! and NOT 8...d6:

  • 8...Be7: White 33% (55 games) — Black leads clearly. Develop piece, delay ...d6.
  • 8...d6: White 54% (147 games) — standard but bad; gives White f4-f5 attack with tempo
  • 8...h5!?: White 35% (79 games) — dynamic, also good, but less solid than Be7

Continuation after 8...Be7 — Austin's winning plan:

9.Qf3 d6 10.0-0-0 0-0 11.g4 b5! — queenside counterattack begins
  • ...b5-b4 undermines White's c3 knight, opens the c-file for rooks, launches queenside attack while White storms on the kingside
  • If White plays f5: respond ...e5 (central counter) or ...Nc6-e5-c4 (knight invasion)
  • If White plays g5 Nfd7: Black proceeds ...b4-bxc3 bxc3, Nc5 to pressure d3 and c3
  • Rook goes to c8 to pressure the c-file; Bishop goes ...Bb7 on the long diagonal

Key rule: Get ...Be7, ...0-0 safety BEFORE playing ...d6. Don't rush ...d6 — it gives White f5 ideas with tempo. 8...Be7 is the critical tempo-saver.

Note on 6.Be2 (26% — also likely): Same plan. Austin plays a6 7.0-0 Nf6 8.f4 Be7 9.Be3 d6 — identical queenside counterplay. White scores 43.5% — still comfortable for Black.

KNOW THIS POSITION. After 5...Qc7 6.Ndb5, Austin must play 6...Qb8! immediately. Never 6...Bc5??
Reyansh: 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 e6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 Nc6 5.Nc3 Qc7 6.Ndb5 Qb8! — 17,134 Lichess games, White 40%. Black is fine after Qb8.

Why Qb8: Nb5 threatens Nc7+ (forking Queen on c7 and Ra8). Qb8 removes the queen from c7, eliminating the fork threat. After 7.a4 (hold Nb5) → Austin plays ...a6 to eventually drive it away, then ...Nf6, ...d6 normal Taimanov. After 7.Nd6+ Ke7 8.Nxf7: position is complicated but White has been greedy — Austin continues active play.

NEVER 6...Bc5?? → 7.Nd6+!! Ke7 8.Nxf7!! — discovered attack on Queen (c7), wins the rook immediately. The Queen on c7 is a tactical liability the moment Ndb5 appears — Qb8 eliminates it.
Reyansh: 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 e6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 Nc6 5.Nc3 Qc7 6.g4 a6 7.g5 d5! 8.exd5 Nxd5

If Reyansh plays 6.g4 (Keres Attack): 6...a6 then strike with 7...d5! immediately. After 8.exd5 Nxd5 9.Nxd5 exd5, Black has the active d5 passer and open files. Reyansh has zero documented Keres Attack games — if he plays it, he is improvising in an unfamiliar line. Austin should be confident. Do NOT play 6...h6 or 6...Nge7 — the central break 7...d5! is the only correct response.

Reyansh: 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 e6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 Nc6 5.Nc3 Qc7 6.g3 a6 7.Bg2 Nf6 8.0-0 Be7 9.a3 d6

6.g3 fits Reyansh's Maróczy background (g3+Bg2). Lichess 1800–2200: 10,365 games, White scores 45.3% — roughly equal. After 8.0-0 (most popular, 7,862 games, White 47%), Austin plays Be7 then d6, then ...b5-b4 queenside expansion. The e6 pawn gives Black more central control than in a pure Dragon. If White plays 8.Nxc6 (803 games, 42%W for White), Austin recaptures 8...bxc6 or 8...dxc6. Do not be surprised by 6.g3 — the plan is identical: ...b5-b4 queenside activity, ...Bb7, knight to e5 or d4.

MARÓCZY BIND PREVENTION — most critical move of the whole game:
After 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3: play e6 (not Nc6). After 4.Nxd4: play Nc6 (not g6).
2...Nc6 then 4...g6 → 5.c4! Maróczy Bind — Reyansh's #1 White weapon (8+ games, 94–97% accuracy). 2...e6 makes it impossible for the entire game.
