Part 1: Austin as White — Rossolimo Sicilian (3.Bb5)

Core challenge: Reyansh is deeply prepared in the Accelerated Dragon (5.c4 Maróczy Bind as White means he knows the pawn structures from both sides). Playing 3.d4 Open Sicilian gives him 4...g6 into familiar Accelerated Dragon/Maróczy territory. The Rossolimo (3.Bb5) cuts his preparation off immediately at move 3.
Austin: 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5 g6 4.0-0 Bg7 5.c3 Nf6 6.Re1 0-0 7.d4 cxd4 8.cxd4 d5 9.e5 Ne4 10.Nc3!

Position assessment: Anti-Rossolimo main line (Lichess masters: 77% of 4,165 games at the 5.c3 position). After 5...Nf6 Austin plays 6.Re1, preparing d4. The critical position is 9.e5 Ne4 — Austin plays 10.Nc3! (264 master games, 57% — main line). Nc3 attacks the Ne4 immediately. Austin must know the main continuation cold:

  • 10...Nxc3 11.bxc3 Na5 12.Bd3 Nc4 13.Bxc4 dxc4 14.d5! — the passed d-pawn is powerful and Austin has the initiative. This is the IQP tabiya Austin knows from Nimzo training. White's c3+d5 pawn structure gives a long-term space advantage.

Critical prep: The 9.e5 Ne4 10.Nc3! position is the most likely actual game position — know the IQP continuation before the game.

Austin: 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5 g6 4.0-0 Bg7 5.c3 e5 6.d3 Nge7 7.Bd2 0-0 8.Na3 d6 9.Nc2

If Reyansh closes the center with 5...e5, Austin plays d3 (solid) and maneuvers Na3-c2-e3 to occupy d5. The f4 advance is a key plan — after preparation, f4 strikes at e5 and opens the f-file. Black's Bg7 is strong defensively, but the closed pawn structure neutralizes some of its diagonal power. Austin aims for a slow strategic game where piece maneuvering dominates.

Austin: 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5 g6 4.Bxc6 dxc6 5.d3 Nf6 6.Nc3 Bg7 7.h3

After 4.Bxc6: Austin gives up bishop pair in exchange for permanently doubled c-pawns. Black has more space and the bishop pair, but the c4-square becomes a permanent White outpost for a knight. Austin targets: Ne2-d4 to dominate the c-file, or Ne2-g3 for kingside play. The structural edge compounds in endgames. Use when wanting maximum clarity, but note that Reyansh may be more comfortable in this technical endgame style — his Mechanics Institute 5W-0L record suggests positional strength.

Reyansh has played 1...c5 in 29/29 documented games vs 1.e4. If for any reason he deviates:

  • 1...e5: Austin plays 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5 (Ruy Lopez) — standard territory
  • 1...e6 (French): Austin plays Advanced French (3.e5) — his documented system
  • 1...c6 (Caro-Kann): Austin plays 2.d4 d5 3.Nc3 dxe4 4.Nxe4 (Exchange CK) — his known setup

None of these are realistic — Reyansh's 1...c5 commitment is absolute in the database.

Part 2: Austin as Black — Taimanov + Maróczy Trap Avoidance

Core insight: Reyansh's Maróczy Bind (5.c4 vs Accelerated Dragon ...Nc6+...g6) is his deepest, most-practiced White system. Playing 2...e6 (Taimanov) permanently prevents it. Reyansh's response to Taimanov (...e6) is standard 5.Nc3 — a system he has less depth in than his Maróczy territory. This move-order change (2...e6 instead of 2...Nc6) shifts the battle to Austin's preparation advantage.
Reyansh: 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 e6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 Nc6 5.Nc3 Qc7

After 5...Qc7 (B47 Bastrikov — Lichess master consensus, 65% of 35,297 games):

  • 6.Be2 Qc7 7.0-0 Nf6 8.Be3 Be7: Main line. Austin aims for ...d6, then ...0-0, then ...b5 queenside expansion. After ...b5, Black plays ...Bb7, ...Nc6-e5 for knight outpost, and ...Rc8 rook activation.
  • Key plan: ...b5-b4 undermines c3 knight; ...Qb6 challenges d4 knight; after White retreats Nxc6 or Ne2, Black has active queenside
  • What NOT to do: Don't allow White to play f4-f5 without responding with ...e5 or ...d5 central counterplay

2...e6 is mandatory — this single move-order change is the entire Black preparation strategy.

DANGER ZONE — Never reach this position:
Reyansh: 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 Nc6?? 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 g6?? 5.c4! — MARÓCZY BIND. Reyansh plays this in 8+ documented games.

Why this is catastrophic:

  • 5.c4 establishes a pawn on e4+c4, denying d5 to Black's pieces forever
  • Reyansh's documented Maróczy opponents: Nalakonda 2090 (drew), Yenamandra (won), Yi Colin, Qi Jenny, Mokeeva, Natraj, Jin David = 8+ games with solid results
  • This is Reyansh's best-practiced White structure — his depth and familiarity far exceeds his other systems
  • Austin would be fighting Reyansh on his strongest home turf

Prevention: 2...e6 (not 2...Nc6) makes 5.c4 Maróczy impossible for the entire game.

Reyansh: 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 e6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 Nc6 5.Nc3 a6 6.g4 Nge7! 7.g5 d5!

If Reyansh plays the Keres Attack (6.g4): Austin responds 6...Nge7 (not 6...h6 which weakens). After 7.g5 d5! strikes back in the center. This is a well-documented equalizing plan. Reyansh has zero documented Keres Attack games — if he plays it, he is improvising. Austin should be confident here. After 8.exd5 Nxd5 9.Nxd5 exd5, Black has active counterplay with the passed d-pawn and good piece activity.
