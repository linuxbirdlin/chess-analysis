§3 — Recommended Weapon: Rossolimo Sicilian (3.Bb5)

Recommended: Rossolimo 3.Bb5. Reyansh responds 3...g6 (confirmed), Austin continues 4.0-0 Bg7 5.c3. Reyansh is 0-2 in this exact structure — lost to Mulay (May 2025) and Atkeson (Oct 2024). The Rossolimo takes him off his Accelerated Dragon theory immediately at move 3, before he can establish his familiar ...Nc6/...g6 structure in a Maróczy context.

Main Line: Rossolimo with 5.c3

Austin: 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5 g6 4.0-0 Bg7 5.c3 — Reyansh's confirmed response in both Rossolimo games. Austin plays c3 to support d4 and build a strong center.
BRANCH A 5...d6 6.d4 (most common — main line)
5.c3 d6 6.d4 cxd4 7.cxd4 Bd7 8.Nc3 Nf6 9.Re1

After 9.Re1: White has a strong central pawn on d4, development advantage, and the e5 advance as a future break. The bishop on d7 is passively placed. Austin's plan: Nc3-d5 (knight outpost), or e5 break to open lines. Black's Bg7 is a long-term strength, but d6 is permanently cramped. This is the most concrete line — Austin should know 10.Be3 (solid) or 10.e5 dxe5 11.d5 (sharp thrust).

BRANCH B 5...e5 — Reyansh tries to close the center
5.c3 e5 6.d3 Nge7 7.Bd2 0-0 8.Na3 d6 9.Nc2

After 5...e5: Black fights for center space but gives up d5. Austin plays d3 (solid) and maneuvers Na3-c2-e3 to occupy d5. This becomes a slow strategic game — Austin should aim for f4 break to undermine e5. The Bg7 on the diagonal to e5 is eventually a target. Austin's superiority lies in the c3-d3 pawn structure giving a safe king and flexible piece play.

BRANCH C 4.Bxc6 — Exchange Rossolimo (sharper)
3.Bb5 g6 4.Bxc6 dxc6 5.d3 Nf6 6.Nc3 Bg7 7.h3

After 4.Bxc6: Austin gives up the bishop for knight, doubles Black's c-pawns, and gets a small but durable structural edge. The c4 square becomes a permanent outpost for White's knight. Black has the bishop pair and more space, but the doubled c-pawns are a chronic weakness. Austin should aim for Ne2-d4 to dominate the c-file and enter a favorable endgame. Use if you want maximum structural clarity.

SIDELINE If Reyansh plays 2...e6 (unlikely — Kan-type)
1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 e6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 a6 5.Nc3

Reyansh plays 1...c5 always, but responds with ...Nc6 +...g6 when given the opportunity. If he plays 2...e6 (Kan/Taimanov structure), Austin continues with standard Open Sicilian — 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 a6 5.Nc3 or 4...Nc6 5.Nc3. This is non-threatening territory for Austin. However, note that Reyansh is very unlikely to play 2...e6 as White — he saves that for his OWN responses when facing 1.e4 as Black (not an issue here).
