§5 — Model Game / Reference Plan

The Najdorf English Attack (6.Be3) is Black's most theoretically rich Sicilian system. The structural plan for Black:

  1. Development (moves 5–9): ...a6, ...e5!, ...Be6, ...Be7, ...O-O. The bishop on e6 is Austin's most important piece — it controls d5 and supports the queenside.
  2. Queenside counterplay (moves 9–14): ...a5 or ...Nbd7 → ...b5 → ...b4. This is the mirror of Austin's own a4 challenge vs Heye's Kan — execute it before White's g4-g5 storm lands (before move 15).
  3. Central activity (moves 14–20): ...Rc8 or ...Nd7-c5 knight maneuver to activate pieces on the queenside files.
  4. Endgame alert: Avoid trading the Be6 bishop. It defends d5, supports the queenside, and controls key dark squares. If Heye plays Nd5 Nxe6, recapture with fxe6 only if it opens the f-file for attack.
Symmetry lesson from the direct encounter: When Austin played White vs Heye's Kan, he used a4 to challenge Heye's b5 expansion (the queenside pawn challenge). Now Austin is Black — play ...b5 before Heye's a4 disrupts him. Move order: ...a6 first (to support ...b5) then ...b5 at the right moment. This is the same structural battle, reversed.
