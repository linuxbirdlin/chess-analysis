Part 1 — Austin as White: Advance French (Never Exchange)

NEVER play Exchange French (3.exd5). Leo won a 134-move endgame in Exchange French structure as Black. This is his comfort zone — symmetrical pawns, technical grinding. Austin's tactical style is precisely wrong for Exchange French against an endgame specialist.
Primary weapon: Advance French 3.e5. Leo's French Defense is well-prepared — he plays it in 100% of games. But the Advance French creates specific structural problems for Black that Leo must navigate actively: the locked light-squared bishop, the ...c5 break decision, and kingside pressure from Austin. Austin already plays Advance French (same system as vs Fernando, vs Justin). Leo has only 1 recorded Advance French game in the dataset (NA U20 R4 vs Fogo 2190) — and he lost that game in 113 moves.

Advance French — Austin's main line

Austin: 1.e4 e6 2.d4 d5 3.e5! c5 4.c3 Nc6 5.Nf3 Qb6 6.a3! Nh6 7.b4 cxb4 8.axb4! — 6.a3! (Austin's prepared 6.a3 Spielmann system — same as vs Fernando and Justin). Blocks …Nb4; prepares b4. Leo's standard …Qb6 threat on d4+b2 is neutralized.
Austin: 6.a3 Nh6 7.b4 cxb4 8.axb4 Nf5 9.Bd3! Nxd4 10.Nxd4 Nxd4 11.cxd4 Qxd4 12.O-O! Qxb4 13.Bb2 Qb6 14.Nd2! — White has tremendous piece activity: Bb2 battery, Nd2-f3-g5, Re1 coming. Black's queen has captured two pawns but is misplaced on b6. Austin's attack is faster.

Key theme: After 12.O-O the position is sharp. Leo's queen is on b4/b6 and his pieces aren't developed. Austin's Nd2-f3-g5 + Re1 + Bb2 battery creates kingside threats Leo cannot easily defuse. This is Austin's tactical style at its best.

Austin: 3.e5 c5 4.c3 Nc6 5.Nf3 Bd7 6.Be2 Nge7 7.O-O cxd4 8.cxd4 Nf5 9.Nc3! Be7 10.g4! — g4! forces the Nf5 to retreat; g4-g5 kingside push creates immediate winning threats. Austin's kingside attack is decisive before Leo develops his queenside.

After 10.g4, Leo must choose: Nh4 (passive) or Nxe3 (trades the strong knight but walks into complications). Austin follows with Ng5 ideas and h4-h5 pawn storm. Leo's light-squared bishop remains locked behind the d5-e6 chain throughout.

Austin: 1.e4 e6 2.d4 d5 3.Nd2 Nf6 4.e5 Nfd7 5.Bd3 c5 6.c3 Nc6 7.Ndf3! — Tarrasch (3.Nd2). After 7.Ndf3, White prepares 8.O-O and then h4-h5 kingside attack. The knight on d2 was temporary — Ndf3 re-routes to attack. Leo must defend actively.

Tarrasch gives Austin a different pawn structure than the Advance but similar strategic themes: bad light-squared bishop for Black, White kingside initiative. Can be used if Leo has prepared Advance French specifically.

Part 2 — Austin as Black: Queen's Indian vs Catalan

Core fact: Leo plays 3.Nf3 specifically to avoid the Nimzo-Indian (3.Nc3 Bb4). Austin's standard weapon (Nimzo) is not available. The correct antidote: Queen's Indian (3...b6) or Bogo-Indian (3...Bb4+) — both force Leo out of his ideal Catalan setup and give Austin active piece play.

Austin's weapon: Queen's Indian

Leo: 1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 e6 3.Nf3 b6! 4.g3 Bb7 5.Bg2 Be7 6.O-O O-O 7...Ne4! — Ne4 is Austin's signature QI move: centralizes the knight, pressures c3, and forces Leo to react. Leo cannot continue his ideal Catalan setup without conceding something.
Austin: 1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 e6 3.Nf3 b6! 4.g3 Bb7 5.Bg2 Be7 6.O-O O-O 7.Nc3! Ne4! 8.Qc2 Nxc3 9.Qxc3 d5! 10.cxd5 exd5 — After ...Ne4 Nxc3 ...d5, Austin has a solid IQP position. The Bb7 fires down the long diagonal; d5 pawn is a strong outpost. Leo's Bg2 now faces Austin's Bb7 in a strategic battle Austin understands.

Plan: After 10…exd5, Austin has an IQP position — Austin's strength (same as Nimzo-Indian). The Bb7 pressures the long diagonal; ...Ba6 is always possible to attack c4 if needed (same as Austin's Nimzo habit). Leo's Catalan setup is broken.

Austin: 1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 e6 3.Nf3 Bb4+! 4.Bd2 Bxd2+ 5.Qxd2 O-O 6.g3 d5! 7.Bg2 c6 8...b6! — Bogo-Indian: after Bxd2+ Qxd2, Leo has the bishop pair but Austin has a solid position. d5+c6 structure is very solid; ...b6 prepares ...Ba6 to attack c4 (Austin's Nimzo habit applies).

The Bogo-Indian gives Austin an immediate piece imbalance (Leo's bishop pair vs Austin's active rook placement). Not as sharp as Queen's Indian but solid. Leo's Catalan g2 bishop stares at nothing useful after Black plays ...d5/...c6.

Austin: 1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 e6 3.Nf3 d5 4.g3 dxc4! 5.Bg2 Nc6! 6.O-O Rb8 7.e3 b5! 8.a4 b4 — Open Catalan: grab the c4 pawn immediately! ...Nc6 + ...Rb8 + ...b5 defends the pawn and creates queenside counterplay. Leo's Bg2 is temporarily pointing at nothing.

The Open Catalan (grabbing c4) is a concrete imbalance — Leo must spend moves recovering the pawn. Austin's ...b5/...b4 creates immediate queenside play. This is the most tactical option vs Leo's Catalan.
