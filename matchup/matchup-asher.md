## Austin as White

### Asher's Black Defense: French Defense (1...e6)

Asher plays the French against 1.e4. Austin's repertoire includes:
- **Advanced French (3.e5)**: Asher plays ...c5 Nc6 (standard Advance). Austin knows this well.
- **Tarrasch (3.Nd2)**: Asher plays both Tarrasch Qxd5 (Chistyakov) and ...Nc6 systems. This is his most-used French sub-system.
- **Exchange (3.exd5)**: Least documented for Asher.

**Recommendation: Tarrasch French (3.Nd2) — Austin's fixed 2026 weapon**

Austin always plays 3.Nd2 Tarrasch vs French. Asher plays the Chistyakov (3.Nd2 c5 4.exd5 Qxd5) in 3+ documented games — Austin prepares it deeper.

**Key preparation:** `5.Ngf3!` (84% of masters, 4,269/5,080 games) → `6.Bc4 Qd6 7.0-0 Nf6 8.Nb3!` (96% of masters, 3,719/3,857 games, Lichess verified). Austin's preparation advantage: knows this to move 15+; Asher likely improvises from move 12.

Austin does NOT play 3.e5 (Advance) or 3.exd5 (Exchange) — both abandoned after 2025.

### Opening Lines

**Tarrasch Branch A (Primary) — Chistyakov / Asher's most-documented:**
```
1.e4 e6 2.d4 d5 3.Nd2 c5 4.exd5 Qxd5 5.Ngf3! cxd4 6.Bc4 Qd6 7.0-0 Nf6 8.Nb3!
```
- After 8.Nb3!: Bc4 eyes f7, Nb3 pressures d4. Black queen awkward on d6. Austin's preparation runs to move 15+.
- Standard plan: 9.Nbxd4 Nxd4 10.Nxd4 0-0 11.Be3 — development with central control

**Tarrasch Branch B — Rubinstein 3...Nc6:**
```
1.e4 e6 2.d4 d5 3.Nd2 Nc6 4.Ngf3 Nxd4 5.Nxd4 Bc5 6.Nb3 Bb6
```
- One documented Asher game in this structure (vs Emerson Johnson, AGCA R1)
- Standard development: Be3/Bd3/0-0 — Austin's preparation deeper than Asher's

### Tactical Themes as White
1. **Chistyakov preparation advantage**: After 3.Nd2 c5 4.exd5 Qxd5, Austin's 5.Ngf3! sequence gives White the initiative. Asher knows the structure; Austin knows the plan deeper (Bc4 → Nb3! → Nbxd4). Austin should play quickly through this preparation — don't slow down.
2. **Bc4 pressure on f7**: In the Chistyakov after 6.Bc4 Qd6 7.0-0, the Bc4 creates permanent pressure on f7. Austin should be aware of the Bc4 pin and tactical shots based on it.
3. **Bishop pair**: In Rubinstein lines (if Asher plays 3...Nc6), Austin gets open game with bishop pair advantage after Be3 and central control.

## Austin as Black

Asher plays 1.e4 and responds to Sicilian with Open Sicilian (2.Nf3 d4) consistently. Against Austin's Sicilian:
- vs ...d6 Najdorf: Asher uses Open Sicilian Nxd4 lines (lost to GM 2526 in B96, beat Kaul 1729 in B90, lost to Albert 2044 in B40)

**Austin's plan**: Play normal Sicilian. Asher is playing the Open Sicilian but lacks preparation depth at 2000+ level. Austin's preparation in Sicilian positions should be stronger.

**Recommendation**: Taimanov Sicilian B47 Bastrikov (1...c5 2.Nf3 e6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 Nc6 5.Nc3 Qc7!) — Austin's fixed Black repertoire. After 5...Qc7!, standard plan: ...Nf6, ...d6, ...Be7, ...O-O, then ...a6 and ...b5 queenside expansion. Asher's Open Sicilian depth ceiling at move 10–12.
