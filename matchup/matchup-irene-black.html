System A — vs Rossolimo Sicilian (4.Bxc6 Exchange) — Primary

A1 — Taimanov Sicilian (Austin's prep choice)
Irene plays 1.e4 → if Austin plays 1...c5 Nf3 Nc6, Irene uses 3.Bb5 (Rossolimo) and then 4.Bxc6 Exchange. The doubled c-pawn structure (Exchange Rossolimo) is well-known: Black accepts the structural damage in exchange for the bishop pair and open b-file.
1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5 e6 4.Bxc6 dxc6 5.d3 Ne7 6.0-0 Ng6 7.Re1 Be7 8.Nbd2 0-0 9.Nf1 Qc7 10.Ne3 c4 11.d4 → minor edge Black (bishop pair)
Austin's plan: After the doubled c-pawns, play ...c4 to fix the queenside, expand with ...e5, and use the bishop pair in the middlegame. Don't panic at the structural imbalance — it's Black's compensation.
Avoid: 4.0-0 Nge7 5.c3 a6 6.Ba4 b5 7.Bc2 → Closed Rossolimo (White keeps bishop, no exchange)
Exchange Rossolimo specialization: Irene specifically uses 4.Bxc6 (not the Closed Rossolimo with Ba4). This means the bishop pair structure arises in every game — Austin must be comfortable with the imbalanced pawn structure. Practice: defend the doubled c-pawns while activating the bishops.
A2 — After 4.Bxc6 dxc6: Queenside expansion plan
The critical plan after the exchange is Austin's queenside play with ...c4, ...b5, ...a5 expansion. Irene will aim to blockade with d4-c3 pawn structure. Austin's bishops compensate for the structural imbalance if activated properly.
4...dxc6 5.d3 f6! (prevent Nd5, control e5) 6.Nc3 e5 7.0-0 Ne7 8.Ne2 Ng6 9.c3 Be7 10.Nf1 0-0 11.Ne3 Nxe3 12.Bxe3 c5 = (symmetrical pawn structure, bishop pair = slightly favorable)
The ...f6 move is critical to prevent Nd5. Without ...f6, White gets a strong knight on d5 that outweighs Black's bishop pair.
