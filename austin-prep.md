## 4-Week Schedule

### Week 1 — Opening Foundations

**Goal:** Get comfortable with the Tarrasch French as White and review Nimzo-Indian lines as Black.

**Daily (20–30 min): Opening study**
- Learn and drill the Tarrasch French: `1.e4 e6 2.d4 d5 3.Nd2`
  - Main line after `3...Nf6 4.e5 Nfd7 5.Bd3 c5 6.c3`
  - Plan: After `...f6 exf6`, play `Nf4` targeting d5, `O-O`, `Qe2`, slow pressure
  - Key rule: Do NOT push f4 or b4 without a clear forcing sequence behind it
- Review Nimzo-Indian `Bxc3+` with `...b6/...Ba6`:
  - `1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 e6 3.Nc3 Bb4 4.e3 O-O 5.Bd3 d5 6.a3 Bxc3+ 7.bxc3 b6 8.Nf3 Ba6`
  - Refresh the `...Ne4` centralization plan and `...N4g3+` sacrifice pattern

**Online practice games (Lichess or Chess.com — Rapid 15+10):**
- Play 2–3 games per day specifically steering toward these openings
- As White: try to reach the Tarrasch French position — even if opponent doesn't play French, practice the setup
- As Black vs 1.d4: Nimzo-Indian every time, specifically the `Bxc3+` variation
- After each game, review the opening phase in the game explorer

**Puzzle work (15 min/day):**
- Puzzle theme: **Pin and knight fork combos** — warm-up, Austin is already good here

---

### Week 2 — Tactical Drilling

**Goal:** Fix the recurring h-file king safety blindspot and sharpen calculation.

**Daily (20–30 min): Themed puzzles**

Focus puzzle themes in this order:

1. **Bishop sacrifice on h2/h7 — both sides**
   - Drill: Find `Bxh2+` and `Bxh7+` sacrifices (the pattern that burned Austin in NAYCC R2)
   - Also drill: How to PREVENT this sacrifice as the defender — learn when to play `h3` early
   - On Lichess: Puzzle themes → "Bishop sacrifice" / "Deflection"
   - On Chess.com: Puzzle Rush / Custom puzzles → bishop sacrifice

2. **Pawn promotion threats**
   - The `d3-d2` pawn march won against Austin in NAYCC R4 (vs Justin) and NAYCC R7
   - Drill: Puzzles where a passed pawn is one move from promotion and requires immediate defense
   - On Lichess: Puzzle theme → "Promotion" and "Advancing pawns"

3. **King safety under open h/g files**
   - Practice recognizing when the h-file is a danger BEFORE it gets opened
   - Drill: Puzzles involving `Rxh7`, `Qxh7+`, `Ng5+h7` attack patterns

**Online games (Rapid 15+10):**
- 2 games per day
- Specific focus: Before each move, ask "is my king safe? is my opponent's king safe?"
- If you lose to a kingside attack, review it immediately and identify the moment it went wrong

**Checkpoint:** By end of Week 2, Austin should recognize `Bxh2+` setups and automatically consider `h3` prophylaxis when playing White.

### Week 3 — Endgame Technique

**Goal:** Convert won endgames and hold drawn ones — Austin dropped multiple half-points here.

**Daily (20–30 min): Endgame study and practice**

**Rook endgames (highest priority):**
- Philidor position (defending a rook endgame — Rook on 6th rank technique)
- Lucena position (winning with an extra pawn — building a bridge)
- Drill: Lichess → Learn → Practice endgames → Rook endgames
- Drill: Chess.com → Learn → Endgames

**Passed pawn endgames:**
- King and pawn vs King — Austin needs to know all cases cold
- Rook vs passed pawn — the key rule: Rook cuts off the king on the long side
- Practice on Lichess endgame practice tool

**Specific patterns from Austin's lost games:**
- Recreate the endgame position from NAYCC R7 (Beegala game) — White's d-pawn on d6, Austin needed to blockade. Practice similar positions.
- Recreate PAM R5 (Vinales game) — rook endgame with Austin being passive. Find the active rook move Austin missed.

**Online games (Rapid 10+5 this week — shorter to reach more endgames):**
- Intentionally trade queens early to force endgames
- Goal: practice converting technical endgames under time pressure

---

### Week 4 — Full Game Integration

**Goal:** Put it all together. Simulate tournament conditions.

**Format: Rapid games 30+0 or 25+10 (closer to tournament time control)**
- 2–3 games per day
- Play the full repertoire: Tarrasch French as White, Nimzo-Indian as Black
- No "takebacks" — treat each game as if it's the real tournament

**Self-review routine after each game:**
1. Was the opening handled correctly? Where did I leave theory?
2. Did I check king safety on every move in the middlegame?
3. Was there a passed pawn I missed (mine or opponent's)?
4. In the endgame — was I active or passive?

**Puzzle work this week:**
- Mixed puzzles (no specific theme) — simulates real game conditions
- Target: Puzzle rating above 1900 on Lichess or Chess.com before the tournament

## Recommended Platforms and Tools
