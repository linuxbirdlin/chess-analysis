The Pawn Push Checklist (2 seconds before every pawn push)
  1. Is my king safe? If any opposing piece eyes the king's sector — make a king move first (Kh2, Kg2, etc.).
  2. What does this pawn do for my pieces? A premature push opens lines you can't control yet.
  3. Can I push next move after a king/rook improvement? One tempo usually won't hurt; getting caught with a wrong pawn structure will.
  4. Against lower-rated opponents: resist the urge to "do something." Maintaining a solid position usually wins; speculative pushes draw or lose.

Rook Endgame Checklist (before each rook move)
  1. Active or passive? If passive (defending, waiting) — find the most active square: 7th rank, behind a passed pawn, or cutting off opponent's king.
  2. Use your passed pawn. Austin had a d4 pawn in PAM R5 and never used it. Push the passer or place the rook behind it.

  4. Trade dominant rooks. If the opponent has a Rc6 or Rd5 dominating the board, offer to trade it — even if the resulting position is slightly worse, removing the dominant piece helps your coordination.

Queen + Rook Back Rank Pattern (memorize)
  1. If opponent's king is on g7/g8/f8 and you have Re1 and a queen — calculate Re8+ first.
  2. The sequence Qd4+ (or Qd8) + Re8+ beats Rf8 with back rank mate in many positions.
  3. Before playing ANY passive pawn move (g3, h3, etc.) in a queen endgame — spend 10 seconds calculating checks and forcing lines first.
  4. "No immediate threats" is not the same as "no tactics." Queen endgames are tactical; look for pins and back rank patterns every move.

King and Pawn Endgame Rules (commit these)
  1. The moment queens come off: king goes to the center. Calculate king to d4/e4 or d5/e5 before any pawn moves.
  2. Count pawn races before they start. If opponent has a passed pawn, count: how many moves to queen? How many for your king to reach? If you're behind — sacrifice another pawn to blockade the passer.
  3. Active king beats passive piece. A knight on c5 plus active king beats a passive king by default. The king must challenge the knight's territory.
  4. F-file is a trap. Austin's king gravitates toward the f-file (near the f-pawn) when the real action is on the queenside. Identify the critical zone first.

Conversion Mindset
  1. Identify the winning piece. In a won endgame, one piece is the key — usually the most active rook or the most advanced passed pawn. Protect and activate it; don't get distracted by other plans.
  2. Don't simplify into unknown endgames. If you don't know how to win R+P vs R, don't trade into it. Keep queens or keep more material on the board.
  3. Against lower-rated opponents: play the "most forcing" move, not the "safest." The safest move often leads to a draw. Forcing moves (checks, pawn pushes, rook infiltration) create problems Black must solve precisely.
