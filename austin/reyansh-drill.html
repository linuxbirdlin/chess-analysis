One-Page Summary for the Board

Reyansh's three exploitable weaknesses — in order of impact:
  1. King navigation (17 errors, 94% non-win: 8D + 8L): Do not accept a draw in K+P endings. His king goes to the center when it should go to the kingside. Games drawn from +4.89, +9.51, +12.34 — and a loss from a completely equal (+0.00) position with one king error.
  2. Preparation boundary, moves 14–28 (15 instances, 53% loss rate, 4 decisive flips): Keep complexity alive through move 25 in the Rossolimo. Reyansh plays natural-looking rook moves (Ra6, Rfe8, Raf8, Rc2) as default when he can't calculate. These are his losing moves. 4 of these directly flipped a positive position to negative in one move.
  3. Missed forcing checks (18 instances — note: 10 occur in wins, 3 draws + 4 losses are the targets): He plays passive moves when checks are available. The Mulay game (Ra3+ from +5.32) is the clearest evidence: missed rook check, position levelled, game lost. The Rossolimo is where this pattern and his preparation gap overlap.
The opening weapon: 1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 Nc6 3.Bb5 (Rossolimo) is already the preparation-boundary trigger. Reyansh is 0-2 in this line (Mulay, Atkeson) — his worst opening as Black by far. After 3...g6 4.0-0 Bg7 5.c3 Nf6 6.Re1 0-0 7.d4 cxd4 8.cxd4 d5 9.e5, Austin is past the master main line with an active pawn center. Reyansh will be improvising from around move 15 onward — right at the start of his error window (Pattern 2 peak: moves 14–28).
