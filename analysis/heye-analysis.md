## 4. Strategic Patterns

### Positional strengths

**Queenside expansion as White:**  
In Italian positions, Heye consistently executes `a4/b4/a5` or `b4/c4` expansions. This creates space advantage and eventually a passed queenside pawn. He knows how to convert this into endgame wins through technical play (PAM R1, Ontario R2, Canada R5).

**Piece coordination in open files:**  
Heye keeps rooks active. In PAM R6 and PAM R8, he used open d-file and f-file pressure to penetrate Black's position. He rarely leaves rooks passive.

**Technical Italian endgame understanding:**  
Multiple games show him converting minor piece endgames cleanly — he understands c-pawn vs b-pawn structures well and doesn't allow opposite-colored bishop draws unnecessarily.

### Positional weaknesses

**Black defense in closed positions:**  
In the Slav, his defensive technique is inconsistent. He draws strong players (Plotkin) but loses to weaker ones (Rodriguez). The Slav games are often long and he blunders in late middlegame/endgame transitions.

**King safety as White:**  
Canada R7 (vs Zachary Dukic): Heye left his king vulnerable after `21.Kf1` — the h2 pawn was unguarded and Black won with `Bxh2`. He sometimes neglects king safety when attacking.
