## Tactical and Strategic Patterns

### Strengths

1. **Technical endgame play** — His best results are in long positional struggles and endgames. The 193-move draw vs Albert (2044), the 127-move win vs WIM Chumpitaz (2358), and the 139-move draw vs FM Lares Flores (2303) all demonstrate extraordinary endgame patience and precision. His 98.3% accuracy in the Albert game is the highest in the dataset.

2. **Clean execution when ahead** — When Emerson gets a stable positional advantage, he converts efficiently. The Zheng win (97.4% acc, 0 blunders, 1 mistake in 85 moves) and the Li Alan win (97.5% acc, 0 blunders, 0 mistakes) show high-quality play in winning positions.

3. **Composure vs elite opposition** — His 95.5% accuracy draw vs IM Fus (2514) and 92.6% vs GM Rodrigue-Lemieux (2529, despite losing) show he plays his best chess under pressure.

4. **Rossolimo Sicilian proficiency** — His Rossolimo White games feature accurate long-game technique. The Chumpitaz win is the clearest example.

### Weaknesses

1. **Sharp tactical complications** — His worst game was vs WGM Paramzina (2269): 83.8% accuracy, 6 blunders, 6 mistakes in 132 moves. In sharp, double-edged middlegames with both kings exposed, he tends to collapse tactically.

2. **Caro-Kann as Black** — His primary Black defense has a 1W-6L-2D record. The Caro-Kann leads to positions where Emerson is passive and defending, and he repeatedly loses in those structures vs 2000+ opponents.

3. **Ruy Lopez stability as White** — Despite being his most-played White opening (C77), his Ruy Lopez results are 2W-2L with the two losses coming against opponents rated 2158 and 1597 — a wide variance suggesting he doesn't have it fully worked out.

4. **Time pressure / late endgame blunders** — The 15-blunder game vs Lonie (1818) in a 187-move struggle shows he can collapse completely in extended endgames under time pressure, even against lower-rated players.

5. **King safety in sharp positions** — Move 35 Rg5 vs Vinayagaram (blunder from even position to -6.04) and move 31 Nd5 vs Julian Joseph (blunder from 0.00 to -3.42) both show vulnerable king-safety decisions in complex positions.
