## 3. Tactical Patterns

### Strengths — motifs Pranav executes well

**Attacking play as White with pawn storms:**
WY R3 (vs Phillips): A model attacking game. Pranav played 1.e4 e5 Italian with d3/c3, then launched g4-h3-Nf1-Ne3-Nd5 maneuver while Black played a passive ...g5 kingside expansion. After Nd5/Nf6# the game ended in a checkmate on move 29. Pranav showed he can calculate concrete sacrificial attacks when Black leaves the king in the center.

**English Attack positional dominance:**
WY R8 (vs Naimov, 1767): Pranav's English Attack with 6.Be3 a5, 10.a5 (stopping ...a5 expansion), then Qd2/f3/Rfc1 built a slow bind. By move 21 Pranav had exchanged queens and entered a completely dominant rook endgame (Nd5/Rc7) winning cleanly.

**Maróczy Bind long-game technique:**
WY R11 (vs Fadeev, 2127): Pranav maintained the Maróczy structure with c4+d5 outpost (Nd5 on move 16). He converted a queenside pawn majority into a passed b-pawn, won d6, and executed a model positional win in 48 moves against a 2127 opponent — his best win of the event.

**Endgame calculation depth:**
WY R9 (as Black vs Ivancic): In a complex rook + bishop endgame, Pranav found the precise ...d2 passed pawn push and ...Bd2-...Be1-...Bxf2 sequence to win the game despite time pressure.

### Weaknesses — tactical and structural blind spots

**Passive defense as Black against 2100+ with space advantage:**
WY R4 (vs Gabadadze, 2147): Pranav's Ruy Lopez as Black devolved into a passive defensive position after Black's ...g4 kingside expansion backfired. White's Nd5/Re6 outpost created unstoppable threats and Pranav was mated in 79 moves.  
WY R5 (vs Rusu, 2191): Lopez again — Pranav tried the ...Na5 ...c5 plan but White's d5 advance locked the center. After a long maneuvering battle (85 moves), Pranav could not hold the knight endgame. Two Ruy Lopez losses in a row suggest a structural pattern problem.  
WY R7 (vs Diaz Guerrero, 2102): Italian as Black — reached a complex middlegame with ...Nf4/...Nh5 maneuver, but after the queens came off, Pranav's endgame was slightly inferior and he lost a 66-move game.

**Misjudging sharp tactical lines under time pressure:**
AGCA Summer R9 (vs Padmanabhan, 1914): Pranav tried an unusual 4.dxc5 sideline against Sicilian (after 3...Nf6 instead of 3...cxd4), got into a poor position, and resigned on move 22. His clock was very low by move 12 (0:32 vs opponent's 1:10), suggesting serious time trouble played a role. This is a notable loss to a lower-rated player.

**Exchange variation misjudgment (Taimanov):**
WY R10 (vs Ragavan, 2148): Pranav used 5.c4 7.Nxc6 against Taimanov, entered a complex IQP-like structure after 8.Bd3, then sacrificed the exchange (Rxd6) on move 20 to win the d-pawn. Despite winning material, he couldn't convert and lost a 73-move game — suggesting overconfidence in a technically winning position.

## 4. Strategic Patterns

**Space control as White:** Pranav uses his pawns to clamp the center (Maróczy c4, English Attack f3+g4) and then maneuvers knights to central outposts (Nd5 is a recurring theme). He prefers long, positional squeeze games as White.

**Queenside expansion:** In the Maróczy Bind games (WY R8, R11), Pranav consistently played a4/b4 or b4 to advance the queenside pawn majority. He understands the strategic plan deeply.

**Weak in defense (as Black):** When put in a passive position as Black, especially in the Ruy Lopez or Italian, Pranav has difficulty finding the right defensive plan. His pieces become uncoordinated. He tends to over-expand (WY R4: ...g4 kingside expansion) rather than consolidate.

**Strong in concrete execution (as White):** Once ahead materially or positionally, Pranav generally converts cleanly. WY R11 (vs 2127) and WY R8 (vs 1767) both showed accurate technical play.

**AGCA Summer R9 — Pranav (W) vs Padmanabhan (1914): Quick loss (0-1, 22 moves)**
