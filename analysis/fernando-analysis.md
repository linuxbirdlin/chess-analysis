## 4. Strategic Patterns

### Positional strengths

**French Defense pawn structures:**
Fernando understands the d5/e6 isolated pawn structure well as Black. He knows to maneuver knights to e4, keep the bishop pair active, and target White's c3/d4 pawn tension. His patience in French Exchange endgames is notable — PAM R3 lasted 72 moves with consistent knight activity.

**Piece coordination and open files:**
PAM R9 showed Fernando coordinating rooks on the 7th rank (`Rb7/Ra7`) to win material after a successful queenside invasion. He knows how to use open files after pawn exchanges.

**Queenside counterplay in French positions:**
Olimpia R4 demonstrated Fernando's understanding of the `...Qb6`, `...cxd4`, `...Nc6` counterplay pattern in the French Advance. After `13.c4?` he correctly played `...Nxc4!` and exploited the structural concession.

### Positional weaknesses

**King safety as White (long castle):**
In his Yugoslav Attack games (Olimpia R7, MEX-ch R6), Fernando commits to long castle but his attacking play can become erratic. Olimpia R7 saw him play `g5`, `h4`, `h5`, then `Bd5??`, `Qxd5??` — a series of inaccuracies giving away the advantage from a clearly superior position.

**Endgame technique in equal positions:**
PAM R8 (French Winawer, 76 moves) and Olimpia R3 (Caro-Kann Panov, 50 moves) both showed Fernando struggling to find the winning plan in technical king-and-pawn + minor piece endgames. His `28...Nxf3+??` in PAM R8 equalized a position where he was ahead by 2+ pawns. He is better at winning clearly superior positions than converting marginally better ones.

**Passive positions as Black vs other systems:**
Ed-National R1 (vs GM Cori 2586, A46 Queen's Indian-like setup) showed Fernando adopting a passive piece arrangement (`...Bb7`, `...Rc6`, `...Rdd6`) that allowed White to dominate the e5 square and eventually win. When Fernando cannot use French patterns, his Black play is less precise.

## 5. Key Game Summaries

**PAM School R9 (May 2026): Cortes Medina, Fernando (2026) vs Wang, Rocky (2079) — 1-0 [A04]**
Fernando's best win of the dataset. He played 1.Nf3/c4 English, maneuvered with `Qa4/Qc2/b4/Nb5/Nxa7`, then used `Qa5/Bxb7/Rxa5` to pick up the a-pawn and create a deadly passed pawn. After `Bxe6 d5 Rbb7` his rooks dominated the 7th rank and Black resigned. Notable because Wang was 53 rating points above Fernando — a clean strategic positional win.

**PAM School R3 (May 2026): Alba Ruiz, Kenneth Damian (1919) vs Cortes Medina, Fernando (2026) — 0-1 [C01 French Exchange]**
Fernando demonstrated French endgame technique over 72 moves. Key phase: knight maneuver `...Nb6–Na4–Nb2–Nc4–Nd6–Ne4` applying constant pressure. After `33.axb4 Ra2` Fernando's rook infiltrated White's position. White's time pressure (less than 1 minute) led to `61.Be1?` and `62.Bf2?!` errors, after which `Rd1+/Rh1/Ne3+/Rxf2/Nxg4` gave Fernando a winning endgame. Strong display of patient maneuvering.

**Olimpia Nacional R4 (May 2026): Martinez Flores, Matias FM (1904) vs Cortes Medina, Fernando (1836) — 0-1 [C02 French Advance]**
Most instructive game for Austin's preparation. After `1.e4 e6 2.d4 d5 3.e5 c5 4.c3 Nc6 5.Nf3 Qb6`, White played the Milner-Barry structure. White's `13.c4?` (mistake, opening the center prematurely) allowed `...dxc4? (inaccuracy — correct was ...Nxc4)` but White's follow-up `16.h5??` was a serious blunder that gave Fernando a solid advantage. Fernando won material with `...Nc6/...Nxd4/...Ke7/...Kf6/...Kf5` king march and eventually won the endgame. Shows Fernando knows the French Advance well enough to beat an FM.

**Ed-National R1 (Aug 2026): GM Cori, Jorge (2586) vs Cortes Medina, Fernando (2130) — 1-0 [A46]**
Fernando's most revealing loss. He played a solid `...Nf6/...e6/...b6/...Bb7` setup vs 1.d4 and held his own through move 29 (position was around +1 for GM). After `30...Qf7??` (allowing `Bh7+ Kf8 Qxg4`) he entered a lost endgame but played resourcefully. GM Cori made several inaccuracies (`36.h4?? 37.hxg5?`) giving Fernando counterplay, but Fernando then played `37...hxg5??` (instead of `Qxg5` drawing) and lost. Shows Fernando can compete with top-level preparation but collapses tactically in time pressure.

**MEX-op R4 (Apr 2026): Cortes Medina, Fernando (2060) vs Valdes Romero, Leonardo (2325) — 1/2-1/2 [Sicilian]**
Fernando's best White result vs strong opposition. After an unusual Sicilian with `4.dxc5 Nxe4 5.cxd6 Nxd6 6.Bf4`, Fernando reached an endgame that eventually drew in a R+B vs R+B ending at move 36. Shows Fernando can maintain defense against players rated 265 points above him.

**Olimpia Nacional R6 (May 2026): Leal Escamilla, Daniel Zinedine (1765) vs Cortes Medina, Fernando (1836) — 1-0 (Fernando loses)**
Fernando's worst result in the dataset. Playing Black in a Sicilian O'Kelly, he reached a completely equal position but was in severe time trouble (under 1 minute). After `39.Re1??` (blunder by White), Fernando played `Nf3!` winning material, but then squandered the advantage with inaccurate queen maneuvers. He eventually blundered into a mating attack at move 51. Lost to a player rated 71 points below him — demonstrates Fernando's unreliability in severe time scrambles.
