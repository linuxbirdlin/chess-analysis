## Tactical Patterns

### Strengths

1. **Najdorf endgame technique (as Black):** In PAM R3 and R8 (Canada Closed), Aarush navigated Najdorf middlegames and endgames with good accuracy. He handles concrete Najdorf positions well against similarly-rated opponents.

2. **Catalan fianchetto middlegame (as White, vs ~1900):** R3 and R9 (Canada Closed) show clean technical play — 93.4% and 98.2% accuracy, minimal CPL. The Catalan is genuinely strong in his hands against opponents he outrates or matches.

3. **Resilience in losing positions:** R4 PAM (won despite 2 blunders, opponent also collapsed) shows Aarush can find resource when behind. He doesn't always resign lost positions passively.

### Weaknesses

1. **h4-h5 kingside attack vulnerability (as Black):** R1 and R2 (Canada Closed) both lost to h4 attacking setups — 80.6% and 91.5% accuracy, 3 blunders in R1 alone. Aarush has no reliable antidote to direct kingside attacks in KID/Sicilian structures.

2. **King-and-pawn endgame technique:** R7 (Canada Closed, 128 moves) and R8 (PAM 2026, 108 moves) both feature 3–4 king-move blunders at 1000 CPL in rook/pawn endings. Aarush consistently walks his king to the wrong squares in theoretical king endgames.

3. **Catalan collapse under pressure from 2000+ (as White):** R4 Canada Closed (85.7% vs 2098), R5 PAM (87.9% vs 2065), R8 PAM (93% but 4 blunders endgame). His Catalan system does not hold up against technical 2000+ players.

4. **Time pressure cascades:** R6 PAM (5B-5M-8I, 100 CPL in a 100-move game vs 1833) shows that under time pressure Aarush's error rate multiplies. The blunder pattern accelerates in games 60+.

5. **Single-move collapses from equality:** R6 Canada Closed: `Rd7??` converted +0.00 to −5.79 (579 CPL). R4 PAM: `Bh6+??` (771 CPL). Aarush sometimes plays one catastrophic move from a completely equal or won position.

## Strategic Patterns

1. **Prefers closed/semi-closed pawn structures:** Catalan/fianchetto is his entire White repertoire. He avoids Open Game structures and sharp tactical battles as White — his style is positional, methodical.

2. **Paradox — best games as Black in sharp positions:** Despite preferring quiet positions as White, his wins as Black come in the sharper Najdorf. This paradox suggests he is better at reactive defense/attack than at proactive positional construction.

3. **Queenside expansion motifs:** When Aarush builds a Catalan, he understands the Rd1/Nc3/b4 queenside expansion ideas. His good White games feature this — but he doesn't always find the key pawn breaks (e5 flagged twice as best in PAM R5).

4. **Endgame technique gap:** The most consistent pattern across all three tournaments is endgame technique failure. Aarush reaches drawn or slightly worse endgames but converts them into losses through king navigation errors.
