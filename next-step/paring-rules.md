

The 2026 North American Youth Chess Championship (NAYCC), held from November 10–17 in Mexico City, governs its pairings and final standings under official FIDE and Confederation of Chess for America (CCA) rules:

💻 Pairing Rules

• System: The tournament is organized as a standard 9-round Swiss system.
• Software: Pairings are generated automatically using Swiss-Manager software, enforcing standard FIDE Swiss-system pairing engines (ensuring players with similar scores are paired against each other, color alternation is maximized, and no two players face each other twice).

📊 Tie-Break Rules

Because direct FIDE titles, norms, and international medals are on the line, final rankings cannot be shared. If two or more players finish the 9 rounds tied on points, the final standings are determined using the following FIDE-standard mathematical tie-break hierarchy:
1. Direct Encounter: The head-to-head result between the tied players. This is applied only if all players tied in the group have played against one another.
2. Buchholz Cut 1: The sum of the scores of a player's opponents, discarding the single lowest-scoring opponent.
3. Buchholz (or Buchholz Cut 2): The sum of the scores of all opponents (or discarding the two lowest-scoring opponents) to evaluate strength of schedule.
4. Greater Number of Wins: The total number of victories achieved over the 9 rounds, where forfeits and byes generally count as wins for this specific metric.
5. Sonneborn-Berger: The sum of the scores of the opponents the player defeated, plus half the scores of the opponents they drew against.
