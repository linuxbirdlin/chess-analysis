6 — Q15 Answer: Counter-Preparation Strategy

"Austin's PAM 2026 games are public. Opponents' coaches can find and study them. How should Austin handle being well-prepared against during the game?"

This is a legitimate concern at this level. Austin's PAM games (July 2026, publicly indexed) show his exact patterns: Bxh7+ Exchange CK, Advanced French pawn choices, Rossolimo tabiya. A well-coached opponent can prepare a specific refutation 12–15 moves deep.

Step 1 — Detect whether you are being prepared against

  • Opponent moves very fast in the opening (first 8–10 moves in under 2 minutes total) — they are retrieving memory, not calculating.
  • Opponent doesn't look at the board — stares at their scoresheet or their own pieces. Prepared moves come from memory, not board scanning.
  • Sharp tactical move played instantly — if a complex-looking sacrifice or quiet move is played in 5 seconds, it was pre-studied, not calculated.

Step 2 — Exit ramps: deviate at the last safe point

Do not deviate at move 1 or 2 — that signals fear. Play normally until you sense preparation, then choose the safe second option:

Step 3 — When behind preparation: play structure, not memory

KEY RULE If you don't know the next move — stop trying to remember and start calculating
  • Ask: "What's the plan for my pieces?" — not "what move do I know here?" A clear positional plan (rook to c-file, bishop to f4, knight to d5) beats trying to recall forgotten preparation.
  • Slow down. If the opponent is moving fast (in prep), Austin should move slowly and think deeply. Their memorized lines run out in 4–8 moves. Austin's calculation outlasts their memory.
  • Solidify before equalizing. A slightly passive but solid position beats desperate tactical gambles to "prove" the prep doesn't work.
  • Identify one concrete threat each turn. Even when behind on the board, Austin's ability to create tactical complexity is the equalizer. Prep is designed for quiet positions — a single tactical shot can shift the game.
Most important principle: Preparation covers positional plans and opening theory — it rarely covers the specific tactical complexities that arise from a real game's specific position. Once 8–10 moves past the prepared line, Austin's calculation ability becomes the deciding factor. Stay in the game, trust your tactics.
