Section F Clock Management Battle Plan

The 45-Second Rule

Never make a move in fewer than 45 seconds in any critical position. This is not about being slow — it's about preventing panic moves. In G/90+30, you have ample time if you use it consistently rather than banking time during non-critical moves and spending nothing on critical ones.

The most common clock mistake: Spending 10–15 minutes on an early middlegame decision, then making tactical moves quickly in the critical window (moves 18–35). This is backwards. Tactics require more time, not less. If you're down to 20 minutes with 20 moves to go, you will blunder — engine data from Pranav, Tarun, Aarush, and Emerson all confirm this pattern. They lost games this way. Don't replicate their mistakes.

2. Tiebreak System — Not Covered Anywhere

Gap: This report says "7/9 puts you in top-3 territory." But the top-3 guarantee is only true if Austin outright leads. In a 33-player Swiss, multiple players may score 7/9 — and the tiebreak criteria determine who gets CM/FM norms. The report never explains how tiebreaks work.

FIDE Swiss tiebreak precedence (most common configuration):
  1. Buchholz (BH): Sum of all opponents' final scores. Playing against higher-scoring opponents = better tiebreak. If Austin beats Tarun early (low BH) and draws Zachary late (high BH), his BH is better than if the order were reversed.
  2. Buchholz-Cut 1 (BH-1): Same as Buchholz, but drop the lowest-scoring opponent. Protects against the one opponent who withdrew early.
  3. Sonneborn-Berger (SB): For each WIN, add opponent's score; for each DRAW, add half. Beating higher-rated players counts more.
  4. Direct encounter: If two players scored identically and played each other, the winner ranks higher.

What this means for Austin's strategy:
  • Do NOT take easy draws vs strong opponents when you have winning chances. A draw vs Zachary (seeded high) gives you +0.5 BH contribution from a high-scoring player. A win gives you +1 BH. But more importantly — a win is what earns the CM norm, not a draw.
  • Do NOT offer draws vs low-rated unanalyzed players. A win over a player who finishes 5/9 is worth more BH than a draw. If you're slightly better, convert.
  • If you finish 7/9 and there are 3 other players at 7/9: Your BH depends on who you beat. Beating Zachary (likely 6–7/9) gives much better BH than beating Danielh (who may finish 4/9). This is not in your control — but the awareness prevents costly draw offers late in the tournament.
