The pattern Austin builds but doesn't execute: In 4 separate games Austin constructed the exact position where Bxh7+ wins — bishop on d3, knight on f3, h7 unguarded — and each time played a different move. The attacking setup was correct; the sacrifice trigger was not fired.

Three Trigger Conditions (ALL must be true before Bxh7+)

  • White's bishop is on d3 aiming at h7
  • White's knight is on f3 ready to leap to g5
  • Black's h7 is defended only by the king — no Nf6 or Ng6 guard

Setup Position (White to play — find Bxh7+)

Exchange Caro-Kann: after 1.e4 c6 2.d4 d5 3.exd5 cxd5 4.Bd3 Nc6 5.c3 Qc7 6.h3 e6 7.Nf3 Bd6 8.O-O Nge7 9.Re1 O-O

Pattern 2 h4–h5 Pawn Storm Sicilian Maróczy / English Attack
✓ Executed: chess4win vs Sean Liu (Oct 10, 2026) · B41 Sicilian Kan, Maróczy Bind

When Black plays ...d6 ...e6 ...a6 ...O-O without blocking the h-file, the h4–h5 storm is structurally available. Austin executed this correctly against Sean Liu — the attack created lasting kingside pressure that won material on move 30.

Trigger Conditions

  • Black has castled kingside and played ...d6 ...e6 ...a6 (Kan/Taimanov structure)
  • Black has NOT played ...h5 to block
  • White has e4+c4 Maróczy pawns and bishop active on d4 or e3
  • White's rook is on h1 (or can reach it via h-file)

Setup Position (after 12.h4 — Black must react)

1.e4 c5 2.Nf3 e6 3.d4 cxd4 4.Nxd4 a6 5.c4 Qc7 6.Nc3 Nc6 7.Be3 Nf6 8.a3 Be7 9.Be2 O-O 10.Rc1 Nxd4 11.Bxd4 d6 12.h4!

♟ Open in Lichess Analysis

The Plan

12.h4! → 13.h5 h6 (Black blocks — concedes g5 outpost) → Qd2 targets h6 · Ng5 outpost available → Rg3/Rh3 battery · Bxh6 when pawn falls → if Black plays 12...h5 (blocks): Ng5! → Bxe7 Qxe7 → Nd5 Qd8 → f4 kingside space → if Black plays 12...h6 immediately: Ng5! Bxg5 hxg5 → open h-file, Qd3 threatens Qxh7#
In the Sean Liu game: 12.h4 Bd7 13.h5 h6 → Austin eventually played 30.Bxh6! winning the h-pawn, then 31.Rg3 with dominant kingside position.

Drill Plan

  1. In the Maróczy Bind setup, if Black has not played ...h5 by move 11, h4 is almost always right. Make it automatic.
  2. Study the h4 timing: it should come before castling if possible (h4 on move 9–11 is strongest when Black's king is already committed).
Pattern 3 IQP Knight Storm — Ncxe4! Nimzo-Indian as Black · 98.1% accuracy win
✓ Executed: NAYCC R3 vs Adithya Chitta (Aug 2025) · E49 Nimzo-Indian Rubinstein

Austin's cleanest tournament win came from correctly identifying the IQP knight sacrifice moment. When White has an isolated d4 pawn and Austin has knights on c5 and f6, the e4 square is chronically weak. Jumping Ncxe4! creates "knight domination" — two knights on e4/e2 that cannot be expelled.

Trigger Conditions

  • White has an isolated pawn on d4 (no pawn on c4 or e4)
  • Austin (Black) has knights on c5 and f6
  • White's queen is on c4 or b3 (overloaded defending d4)
  • e4 is unguarded or defended only by the queen

Setup Position (Black to play — find Ncxe4!)

After 1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 e6 3.Nc3 Bb4 4.e3 O-O 5.Bd3 d5 6.a3 Bxc3+ 7.bxc3 b6 8.Nf3 Ba6 9.cxd5 exd5 10.Bxa6 Nxa6 11.Qd3 Qc8 12.O-O c5 13.Nd2 Qb7 14.f3 Rfe8 15.Re1 Rad8 16.e4 dxe4 17.fxe4 cxd4 18.cxd4 Nc5 19.Qc4 ← Black to play

♟ Open in Lichess Analysis

The Winning Sequence

19...Ncxe4! (temporarily loses a pawn but gains outpost dominance) 20.Nf3 Rc8 21.Qb5 Nc3! (forks queen + Re1) 22.Rxe8+ Rxe8 23.Qf1 Ne2+! 24.Kh1 Ne4 (two knights dominate e4 + e2 — White's pieces paralyzed) 25.Qe1 Qd5 26.Be3 N4g3+! 27.hxg3 Qh5+ 28.Nh2 Rxe3! 29.g4 Qd5 30.Rc1 h6 31.Nf3 Qe6 32.Qf2 Nxc1 0-1

Resulting Domination Position

♟ Knight domination after Ne2+
Why it works: The IQP structure has no pawn on e3 or f3 to kick the knight. Once Nc3 forks the queen, White has no time to reorganize. Two coordinated knights on e4 and e2 attack d4, f4, c3, g3 simultaneously — a structural bind.

Drill Plan

  1. In the Nimzo-Indian Bxc3+ with ...b6/...Ba6: after White plays ...Qc4, always check if Ncxe4! works.
  2. Verify: is there a pawn on e3 or f3? If no → Ncxe4 is almost always correct.
  3. Study the Nc3 fork (step 3 above) — it's the key tactical shot that makes the sacrifice pay off.
Pattern 4 Rook Battery + f4 Storm Nimzo-Indian as White · IQP vs IQP
✓ Executed: chess4win vs Laksshana Deepak (Oct 11, 2026) · E46 Nimzo-Indian Normal

When Austin has Ne5 (outpost), f4 (space), and can swing the rook to f3 or h3, the rook battery into a kingside attack is a recurring theme from the Nimzo-Indian as White. The position has a "rook lift" structure: the f-rook swings to h3, the Ne2 repositions to g3, and the h4–g5 breakthrough follows.

Trigger Conditions

  • Austin has Ne5 (outpost — Black's knight is on e4 as counterpart)
  • Austin has played f4 (space grab, restricts Black's e5 break)
  • Austin's f1-rook is available to swing to f3 or h3
  • Black's king is castled short with pawns on g7/h7 intact

Setup Position (White just played Rf3)

After 1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 e6 3.Nc3 Bb4 4.e3 O-O 5.Bd2 b6 6.Nf3 Bb7 7.Bd3 d5 8.cxd5 exd5 9.Rc1 a6 10.O-O Bd6 11.Ne5 c5 12.f4 Nc6 13.Ne2 Ne4 14.Rf3!

♟ Open in Lichess Analysis

The Plan

14.Rf3! (battery prep — rook activates toward h3) → g4 (space grab — Black's Ne4 will be pushed) → Rh3 (rook targets h7) → Ne2→g3 (knight joins the attack, supports h4) → g5 (breakthrough — open g-file or h-file) Key moment in game: 18.g4 cxd4 19.Bxg6! fxg6 20.exd4 → open f-file, knight on g3 next
The rook lift pattern (Rf1–f3–h3): This is the same mechanism Austin uses in the French Tarrasch with h4 — the f-rook slides along the third rank to the h-file. In any structure where Black's king is castled short and Austin has f4 + a bishop on d3, ask: "Can I play Rf3 → Rh3?"

Drill Plan

  1. After Ne5 + f4 are in place, the next question is always: "Can I swing the rook to h3?"
  2. Practice the Ne2→g3 repositioning — it's the key piece that ties the attack together.
Pattern 5 Active Defender Scan — Forcing Checks CRITICAL · Recurring miss in 6+ games
✗ Recurring miss: personal study games + NAYCC + PAM
The single costliest habit: In winning positions (+2.0 or better), Austin repeatedly plays a positional or passive move when a forcing check or sacrifice was available and winning. Why It Happens

In a winning position, Austin shifts to consolidation mode — playing solid, non-committal moves to preserve the advantage. The engine wants forcing mode — the one concrete move that closes the game out immediately. The passive move feels "safe" but allows the opponent to regroup.

The common thread: In every case above, the winning move was a check or sacrifice that forced the king to a specific square. Austin saw the passive alternative first and played it without checking for the forcing option.
⚡ Before Every Move in a Winning Position (+2.0 or better)

Run this 4-step scan before playing your candidate move. It should take 10–15 seconds. If any step fires, play that move — do not default to the positional alternative.

  1. Bxh7+ check. Is my bishop on d3 and my knight on f3? Is Black's h7 defended only by the king?
    → If YES to all three: play Bxh7+ Kxh7 Ng5+ and calculate escapes.
  2. Forcing check scan. Can any piece give check that forces the king to a bad square?
    → Try every checking move mentally. If one wins material or forces checkmate: play it.
  3. Free material capture. Is there a piece hanging that I can take — check, then capture?
    → Check tactics: forks, pins, skewers, discovered attacks.
  4. Only if none of the above fire: play your positional candidate move.
    Consolidation is correct only when no forcing option exists.
