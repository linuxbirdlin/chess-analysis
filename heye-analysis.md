## 3. Tactical Patterns

### Strengths

**Italian endgame conversion:**  
Heye consistently converts Italian positions when he gains a structural advantage. PAM R8 (vs Simbler 2034) showed a clean endgame: after neutralizing early complications, he advanced a c7 pawn to queen while Simbler's rook was passive (move 37 `c7` ending the game). PAM R1 (vs Rincon) ended with a mating net involving a passed c-pawn and rook battery. He is very comfortable with pawn-endgame technique in Italian structures.

**Sicilian attack execution:**  
PAM R6 (vs Sen, B75 Dragon Yugoslav): Heye got White's queen to e7, splitting Black's rooks, then used a rook lift to d6/c6 to pick up material. The attack finished with a tactical sequence `Rf7–Rf8–Rd6–Rxc6` forcing resignation. He understands how to deliver the Yugoslav Attack from inside-out.

**Beating higher-rated players with counterplay:**  
Ontario R4 (vs Vettese 2410, C47 Four Knights): after White's inaccuracy `13.g4??`, Heye calculated `Nxg4+/Qh4+` immediately and won two pieces for a rook. He then conducted a technical endgame conversion over 40 moves, trading into a won K+P+N vs K+P+N endgame. This shows excellent calculation under pressure when ahead.

Canada R4 (vs Plotkin 2382, D23): held a draw through solid defense as Black, never losing the thread despite long endgame pressure.

### Weaknesses

**Tactical miscues when under pressure with Black:**  
PAM R9 (vs Rodriguez 1971): Heye had a worse but playable position when he played `23...Kg8??` (blunder — correct was `23...c5`), allowing `24.Re8+ Kh7 25.Be4+ g6 26.Ne2` and White's pieces dominated. Lost to a 1971-rated player in a position that shouldn't be lost at 2065 level. This is his most glaring weakness: Black calculation under pressure.

PAM R7 (vs Diaz Guerrero CM 2209): two tactical errors in succession (`24...Qe7??` allowing `25.exf7` and `28...Qxf7??` losing the bishop) in a position that was around equal. Both blunders happened in the time trouble zone (under 25 minutes). Pattern: Heye's Black calculation degrades in time pressure.

**Aggressive White h4 attempts can backfire:**  
Ontario R5 (vs Razin 2329, C16 French Winawer): Heye played `5.h4 b6 6.h5 h6 7.Rh3` — a sharp anti-French system. The position became -1.7 for White by move 10 when Black simply counterattacked. Against strong French players, Heye's h4 attack is premature and allows well-prepared opponents to equalize comfortably.

**Canada Closed R7 (vs Zachary Dukic 2230):** Playing White in C55 Two Knights, Heye got a slightly favorable position then lost material in the endgame after `21.Kf1 Bxh2 22.Nd5 Qa6+` — a tactical sequence he missed.

## 4. Strategic Patterns

### Positional strengths

**Queenside expansion as White:**  
In Italian positions, Heye consistently executes `a4/b4/a5` or `b4/c4` expansions. This creates space advantage and eventually a passed queenside pawn. He knows how to convert this into endgame wins through technical play (PAM R1, Ontario R2, Canada R5).

**Piece coordination in open files:**  
Heye keeps rooks active. In PAM R6 and PAM R8, he used open d-file and f-file pressure to penetrate Black's position. He rarely leaves rooks passive.

**Technical Italian endgame understanding:**  
Multiple games show him converting minor piece endgames cleanly — he understands c-pawn vs b-pawn structures well and doesn't allow opposite-colored bishop draws unnecessarily.

### Positional weaknesses

**Black defense in closed positions:**  
In the Slav, his defensive technique is inconsistent. He draws strong players (Plotkin) but loses to weaker ones (Rodriguez). The Slav games are often long and he blunders in late middlegame/endgame transitions.

**King safety as White:**  
Canada R7 (vs Zachary Dukic): Heye left his king vulnerable after `21.Kf1` — the h2 pawn was unguarded and Black won with `Bxh2`. He sometimes neglects king safety when attacking.
