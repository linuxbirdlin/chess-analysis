The Bxh7+ sacrifice works when:
1. White has a bishop on d3 (or c2) pointing at h7
2. Black king is on g8 (castled) or e8 (uncastled)
3. White has a knight that can come to g5 after Qh5+
4. No Black piece covers g5 after ...Kg8

Bxh7+! Kxh7 Qh5+ Kg8 Ng5 — threatens Qh7# and Nxf7. Black is lost.

If Black plays 16...Re8 (trying to defend):
17.Qxf7+! Kh8 18.Qh5+ Kg8 19.Qh7+ Kf8 20.Qh8#
Training exercise Before each training game or analysis session: set up a Taimanov English Attack position and ask: "Is Bxh7+ available?" Conditions: bishop on d3, knight near g5/f3, Black king on g8/e8, no Black piece on f5 or h5. If all four hold, calculate it.
When to play g5: After g4 has been played and Black's knight is on f6. g5! forces Nd7, misplacing the knight and gaining kingside space in one move.

After g5 Nd7: f4 — continue the storm. f4–f5 is the plan.
Or: h4 — prepare h5, threatening h6 pawn break.

Do NOT play g5 if Black hasn't played ...Nf6 yet — it's wasted tempo. Play Qd2 + O-O-O first, then g5 when the moment is right.
The Nd5 outpost: After queenside castling and g4, White often has a knight on c3 or e2. Nd5 is available when the e6 square is exchanged away or Black plays ...d6.
